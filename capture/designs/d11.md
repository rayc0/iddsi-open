I have the sealed set, the task, and the capture protocol. One material discrepancy had to be resolved before designing: `sealed.txt` for d11 contains **one** base food (bf25, 5 specimens), not the 12×5 the task text describes — so all contrasts must come from bf25. I've read bf25's five specimen cards and built the design around that constraint.

SEALED_SET: d11/sealed.txt contains only bf25 (stewed_hairy_gourd, cohesive_mixture) × L3–L7 = 5 specimens. All 12 contrasts are therefore intra-food; no cross-food pairs are possible, so this batch cannot separate texture from food identity (a model could score well by recognising the prep route, not the texture). Verdict is about bf25 only.

CONTRASTS:

pair 1: food=bf25 level=L3↔L4
  A: L3 base per card — blend 30–45 s, 4 mm sieve, 3.0 g thickener/100 ml, +50 ml water, rest 60 s, re-stir. looks: pale green-beige, uniform, matte-to-slightly-glossy, no fibre, no specks; levelled flat into a 60 mm disc inside a plain white bowl, top-down still, shot within 10 s of levelling.
  B: L4 base per card — 300 g drained 2 min in sieve, blend 60 s (scrape at 30 s), 4 mm sieve, hot water cut to 20 ml, no thickener. looks: identical colour, sheen, uniformity and 60 mm levelled disc in the same bowl; bowl wall suppresses the slump cue.
  A measures: 10 ml syringe flow — 8.5–9.5 ml remaining at t=10 s; fork-drip = continuous slow ribbon through tines; spoon-tilt = slow continuous stream off the tilted spoon.
  B measures: fork-drip = sits in a mound above the tines, zero flow in 10 s; spoon-tilt at 90° = slides off as one cohesive mass with <5% residue; no separate liquid pools.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: same batch, colour, sheen and surface geometry — the defining property is flow, which is not a static attribute of anything in the frame.
  STRENGTH: strong (this is the L3/L4 viscosity boundary itself, isolated to one variable with all else held; the only caveat is that bf25's L4 relies on native mucilage, so the B side may need the card's thickener fallback, which would widen the gap slightly).

pair 2: food=bf25 level=L3↔L4
  A: L4 built to look thin — puree blended with 60 ml of the stewing liquor plus 4.0 g thickener/100 ml; glossy, translucent, wet-looking. looks: the wetter, glossier, more translucent of the two.
  B: L3 built to look thick — 300 g drained 2 min, 3.0 g thickener/100 ml, only 40 ml water, folded dry; matte, opaque, dry-looking. looks: the matte, opaque, drier of the two.
  A measures: fork-drip = mound above tines, no flow in 10 s; spoon-tilt 90° = one cohesive mass, no pooling.
  B measures: 10 ml syringe flow — 9.0 ml remaining at t=10 s; fork-drip = continuous slow ribbon.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: the visible wetness/gloss/opacity proxy points the wrong way — the specimen that looks thinner is the one that holds a mound.
  STRENGTH: strong (the most diagnostic pair in the batch: it breaks the gloss↔thickness correlation a photograph would otherwise let a model exploit, and it needs no exotic rheology — only liquor content vs thickener dose).

pair 3: food=bf25 level=L3↔L4
  A: L3 by hydration state — 3.0 g thickener/100 ml added dry to the sieved puree (incomplete hydration, lower effective viscosity), 60 ml water. looks: smooth, pale green-beige, matte, indistinguishable from B.
  B: L4 by concentration — no thickener; puree simmered 3 min to concentrate native mucilage, then sieved. looks: identical colour, smoothness and sheen.
  A measures: 10 ml syringe flow — 8.5–9.5 ml remaining at t=10 s; fork-drip = continuous ribbon.
  B measures: fork-drip = mound above tines, no flow in 10 s; spoon-tilt 90° = cohesive mass.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: neither hydration state nor mucilage concentration changes colour, smoothness or particle content — nothing in the still moves.
  STRENGTH: moderate (physically real and genuinely invisible, but the viscosity gap between a partly hydrated 3 g/100 ml dose and concentrated native mucilage is narrow — without a pilot this pair risks both sides landing on the same side of the boundary).

pair 4: food=bf25 level=L3↔L4
  A: L3 — identical base to B plus the protocol's own correction increment: 3.0 g thickener/100 ml, +50 ml water. looks: identical to B in every respect.
  B: L4 — same base, same 50 ml water, plus one extra 0.5 g thickener per 100 g (the smallest weighed step the protocol permits). looks: identical to A.
  A measures: 10 ml syringe flow — 8.5–9.5 ml remaining at t=10 s; fork-drip = continuous slow ribbon.
  B measures: fork-drip = mound above tines, no flow in 10 s; spoon-tilt 90° = cohesive mass.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: 0.5 g per 100 g is the protocol's own correction increment — the tightest physical difference the desk workflow can produce, and it is entirely invisible.
  STRENGTH: strong as a boundary probe, conditional (requires a pilot specimen to confirm 0.5 g/100 g actually straddles the holds-a-mound line for bf25; if it does not, the operator must widen to 1.0 g/100 g and the pair degrades toward pair 1).

pair 5: food=bf25 level=L4↔L5
  A: L4 per card — sieved puree, 20 ml hot water, no thickener. looks: smooth matte pale green-beige disc, no visible grain at the protocol framing.
  B: L5 by dicing, not mashing — gourd cut to exactly 4.0 mm cubes and folded with a minimum of its own fine puree to coat, no free liquid. looks: same colour, same gloss, same bulk geometry; a fine uniform 4 mm grain is visible only in the fork-press close-up at the tine edges.
  A measures: fork-drip = mound above tines, no flow in 10 s; spoon-tilt 90° = one cohesive mass, no residue.
  B measures: particle gauge — max piece 4.0 mm on a 5 g scoop against the 4 mm sieve/ruler; spoon-tilt 45° = loose moist mound, no run-off; fork-drip = nothing through the tines in 5 s.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: both are moist, uniform, same-coloured material at the same gloss; the only difference is a 4 mm grain at the edge of what a hero shot resolves.
  STRENGTH: strong (this is the L4/L5 absolute particle-size boundary, and the dice route makes the specks geometrically regular and the same colour as the matrix, so no contrast cue is available).

pair 6: food=bf25 level=L4↔L5
  A: L4 that looks wet — pureed with 60 ml stewing liquor + 4.0 g thickener/100 ml; glossy, wet, translucent. looks: the wetter-looking specimen.
  B: L5 that looks dry — hand-mashed to ≤4 mm, drained 30 s, no added liquid, folded dry; matte, opaque, crumbly. looks: the drier, more "chopped"-looking specimen.
  A measures: fork-drip = mound above tines, no flow in 10 s; spoon-tilt 90° = cohesive mass, no pooling.
  B measures: particle gauge — max piece 4 mm; spoon-tilt 45° = loose moist mound, no run-off; fork-drip = nothing through the tines in 5 s.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: moisture and gloss point the wrong way relative to the true boundary, so neither can serve as the cue.
  STRENGTH: strong (deliberately inverts the wet↔smooth correlation; the L5 side is at some risk of reading as an under-sieved L4 during QC, which the operator must not "fix").

pair 7: food=bf25 level=L4↔L5
  A: L4 per card — sieved smooth. looks: uniform matte pale green-beige.
  B: L5 with no contrast cue — 3–4 mm mash folded into an equal mass of the same fine sieved puree, so the particles share the continuous phase's colour and gloss exactly. looks: identical to A except a faint granular texture detectable only in the fork-press close-up.
  A measures: fork-drip = mound above tines, no flow in 10 s; spoon-tilt 90° = cohesive mass.
  B measures: particle gauge — max piece 4 mm; spoon-tilt 45° = loose moist mound, no run-off; fork-drip = nothing through the tines in 5 s.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: identical colour, gloss and bulk geometry; the difference is a 3–4 mm granularity inside a same-coloured matrix.
  STRENGTH: moderate (the hardest pair to see, but the fold is easy to get uneven — an uneven B side weeps free liquid and fails the L5 no-pooling check, which would turn the pair into a QC failure rather than a contrast).

pair 8: food=bf25 level=L5↔L6
  A: L5 — mash pressed through the 4 mm sieve; particles 3–4 mm. looks: fine moist pale green-cream granular mound.
  B: L6 at the small end — diced to exactly 8.0 mm cubes, drained 30 s. looks: coarser moist granular mound, same colour, same gloss, no free liquid on either.
  A measures: particle gauge — max piece 4 mm; spoon-tilt 45° = loose moist mound, no run-off; fork-drip = nothing through the tines in 5 s.
  B measures: fork-pressure at L6 — cube squashes under thumb-to-thumbnail-blanch pressure and does not rebound; 100 mm ruler check — every piece ≤15 mm; spoon-tilt 45° = cubes sit in a pile, none slide off.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: only a 2× size ratio between 4 mm and 8 mm, at identical colour, moisture and gloss, both reading as "lumpy moist gourd".
  STRENGTH: strong but fiducial-dependent (this is the pair that most needs the ruler and checkerboard masked from the model input — unmasked, 8 mm vs 4 mm is directly measurable off the fiducial and the pair becomes trivial).

pair 9: food=bf25 level=L5↔L6
  A: L5 folded dry — mashed to ≤4 mm, drained 30 s, no added liquid. looks: matte, crumbly, "chopped".
  B: L6 wet — 8–15 mm cubes in a little of their own liquor, drained only 10 s. looks: glossy, wet, cubes sliding slightly, "smoother and wetter".
  A measures: particle gauge — max piece 4 mm; spoon-tilt 45° = loose moist mound, no run-off.
  B measures: fork-pressure — squash, no rebound; ruler check ≤15 mm; spoon-tilt 45° = pile holds, no cube slides off; fork-drip = cubes stay on the fork (minor free-liquid drip is permitted at L6).
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: the wetter-looking specimen is the coarser one, inverting the moisture↔size cue.
  STRENGTH: moderate (the free liquid on the B side is protocol-legal at L6 — "a small amount of thin liquid is acceptable" — which makes it a legitimate but leaky visual cue; a model keying on wetness gets B right and A wrong, which is still informative).

pair 10: food=bf25 level=L5↔L6
  A: L5 with cut-looking particles — coarse hand-mash at 4 mm holding irregular angular shapes. looks: more "diced" and regular-edged than B.
  B: L6 with collapsed-looking pieces — 10 mm cubes of long-stewed gourd that slump and lose their edges. looks: more "mashed" and irregular than A.
  A measures: particle gauge — max piece 4 mm; spoon-tilt 45° = loose moist mound, no run-off.
  B measures: fork-pressure — squash, no rebound; ruler check ≤15 mm; spoon-tilt 45° = pile holds.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: edge regularity and apparent firmness are inverted relative to true particle size, and both are soft, moist, same-coloured.
  STRENGTH: moderate (a good inversion, but the slumped 10 mm cubes sit close to the L6 fail mode "cube collapses to puree on contact" — if they crumble under the fork the B side fails QC and the pair is lost).

pair 11: food=bf25 level=L6↔L7
  A: L6 — cubes diced to exactly 15.0 mm. looks: distinct soft pale green-cream cubes with clean edges, moist surface.
  B: L7 — chunks "as stewed" from a batch stewed in small pieces, 18–20 mm. looks: the same soft pale green-cream chunks with clean edges, same gloss.
  A measures: fork-pressure — squashes and does not rebound; ruler check — every piece ≤15 mm.
  B measures: fork-pressure — the chunk does NOT fully squish flat between fork and plate; spoon-tilt 45° = sits on the spoon without flowing or dripping off; fork-drip = no liquid runs off between the tines.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: a 15 mm vs 18 mm difference at identical colour, gloss and edge quality; the real discriminator is the fork-pressure outcome, which no still shows.
  STRENGTH: moderate (narrow but real — as a *preparation* contrast 15 vs 18 mm is only a 20% gap, but as a *photograph* contrast it is strong because the L6/L7 discriminator is squash-vs-hold, an action, not an appearance).

pair 12: food=bf25 level=L6↔L7
  A: L6 — 12 mm fork-tender cubes that hold crisp diced edges. looks: firm, regular, "neatly cut".
  B: L7 — 20 mm long-stewed chunks that are very soft and slump at the edges. looks: softer, less regular, more "collapsed".
  A measures: fork-pressure — squash, no rebound; ruler check ≤15 mm.
  B measures: fork-pressure — chunk does not fully squish flat; spoon-tilt 45° = sits without flowing off.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: apparent firmness and edge regularity are inverted relative to piece size, so "looks firmer" points to the smaller pieces.
  STRENGTH: weak (bf25's L6 and L7 forms differ mainly in size, and once the 100 mm ruler is in frame the pair is directly separable; even masked, the edge-collapse cue on the B side leaks — the honest read is that for a single cohesive food like bf25 the L6/L7 boundary is largely visible).

REPEATS: bf25_stewed_hairy_gourd_L3 ×3 — spread of the syringe reading at t=10 s around the 8–10 ml window (the only continuous numeric reading in the set) plus thickener-dose and 60 s rest-time effects; the widest expected noise in the batch.
REPEATS: bf25_stewed_hairy_gourd_L4 ×3 — fork-drip and spoon-tilt are pass/fail, so the repeats expose how often the same prep lands on the wrong side of the holds-a-mound boundary, plus sieve/fibre-removal repeatability and whether separate thin liquid appears after resting.
REPEATS: bf25_stewed_hairy_gourd_L5 ×2 — max particle size on the 5 g scoop against the 4 mm gauge, and the loose-mound / no-run-off spoon-tilt outcome; exposes hand-mash particle-size variability (bf25's L5 card has no blender at this level, so this is entirely operator-dependent).
REPEATS: bf25_stewed_hairy_gourd_L6 ×2 — max piece size on any axis against the 100 mm ruler, and the squash-no-rebound fork-pressure outcome; exposes knife-dicing size spread and cook-doneness drift between batches.
REPEATS: bf25_stewed_hairy_gourd_L7 ×2 — fork-pressure outcome (does the chunk fully squish flat) and free-liquid pooling; exposes stewing-time variability, the dominant L7 noise source for a food whose L7 is "as stewed" with no size control.

WEAKEST_CONTRAST: pair 12 — bf25's L6 and L7 forms differ mainly in piece size, and the 100 mm ruler is in every frame, so the pair is separable by reading the fiducial rather than by any texture judgement; even with the fiducial masked, the slumped-edge cue on the L7 side still leaks. It is also the pair with the least physical headroom, since "as stewed" gives no size control on the L7 side.

VERDICT: SUPPORTED — unconditionally for the L3/L4 viscosity boundary (pairs 1–4: a still carries no flow information, and pair 2 inverts the gloss proxy so a model keying on wetness is actively misled); for L4/L5 through L6/L7 (pairs 5–12) only if the 100 mm ruler and 15 mm checkerboard are masked from the model input, because with the fiducial in frame the absolute 4 mm / 15 mm / 15 mm sizes are directly readable and those pairs become solvable by scale-reading rather than texture. Unmasked, this batch is INSUFFICIENT for the particle-size half of the hypothesis — masking the fiducial is the single change that decides it.
