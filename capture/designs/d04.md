  bf08_steamed_sweet_potato_L4 x 1 — milling duration (60 s vs 90 s vs 120 s): the fork-drip pass/fail and the spoon-tilt hold time are both sensitive to how long the blender runs.
  bf08_steamed_sweet_potato_L4 x 1 — rest/set time between plating and testing: L4 is defined by holding a soft shape, and shape-hold changes over the first 10 minutes.
  bf08_steamed_sweet_potato_L4 x 1 — fat source at matched gloss (butter vs oil vs cream): tests whether the L4 call tracks sheen or viscosity, since both sides photograph identically.
  bf08_steamed_sweet_potato_L5 x 1 — dice-size operator variability at the 4 mm boundary: a 3 mm vs 5 mm hand-cut flip moves the specimen across the L4/L5 line.
  bf08_steamed_sweet_potato_L5 x 1 — moisture of the minced mass (drained vs spoon-moist): L5 must be moist, and both spoon-tilt hold and fork-mash effort move with it.

WEAKEST_CONTRAST: pair 2 (same recipe at 40 °C vs 18 °C). I trust it least because it is invisible for a reason that has nothing to do with the hypothesis — temperature is not part of how any IDDSI level is defined, so a flip here demonstrates that an unmeasured nuisance variable exists, not that the level-defining property is absent from the image. Serving temperature also cannot be held to better than a few degrees across a shooting session, so the two sides may not differ as much as intended. Runner-up: pair 10, because 15 mm vs 18 mm is a genuine visible difference once a fork is in frame, so it understates how hard the L6/L7 boundary really is — pair 11 is the honest version of that boundary.

VERDICT: INSUFFICIENT. No measurements exist yet — this is a design, and a design cannot support or refute a physical claim. Two concrete reasons it is not merely premature: (1) sealed.txt contains one base food, not twelve, so all 24 specimens come from bf08_steamed_sweet_potato and there is no cross-food colour, gloss or shape variance to tell "this food's level is invisible" from "orange glossy purees are hard"; (2) docs/CAPTURE_PROTOCOL.md is not present, so the test readings above are specified against the summary in the task text and the cut-effort ordinal is my addition, not the protocol's. The decision rule that would make it decisive: for each pair, hold presentation, colour and gloss fixed, score both sides blind, and call the hypothesis SUPPORTED if the model assigns the same level to both sides on the strong pairs (1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 11) at a rate well above the repeat-derived noise floor, and REFUTED if it separates them reliably. With only bf08 available, that rule can be run but only answers the question for one food — hence INSUFFICIENT rather than SUPPORTED, which is the direction the design predicts.

CONVENTIONS: spoon-tilt = tilt to 45° from horizontal, hold 10 s, record time to first movement, spread in mm, % mass remaining; fork-drip = ~15 g on horizontal tines for 10 s, record mass dripped through; syringe = 10 ml ejected over 10 s, record ml remaining (more remaining = thicker; L3 window is 8–10 ml); fork-pressure = press until the nail bed blanches, max 3 presses, record penetration plus cut-effort ordinal 0 (prongs pass freely) to 3 (cannot be cut with the fork edge). The ordinal is an addition for these contrasts and should be validated against the real protocol before use.

LIMITS: sealed.txt lists only bf08_steamed_sweet_potato_L3–L7 (5 specimens), against the 12 foods × 5 levels = 60 the task describes; the other 11 base foods are not in the file, so every contrast and repeat above is drawn from bf08 and all pairs are within-food. A single food also means the 12 repeats cannot separate operator variability from food-specific variability. Pair 12 relies on camera angle rather than physics, pair 6 is not a minimal pair, and pair 11's firm side is slightly paler and less glossy — a residual visual leak I would try to close by matching variety and shooting both sides within the same minute. I would also add, if the set ever reaches 12 foods, a within-level control pair: two L3 preps at 8.0 ml and 9.9 ml remaining at t=10 s, both passing L3, to measure how much within-level viscosity spread is visible at all.
