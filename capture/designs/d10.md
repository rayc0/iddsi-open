SEALED-SET NOTE: `sealed.txt` for d10 exposes **5** specimens (`bf24_braised_winter_melon_L3`–`L7`), not the 12 base foods × 5 levels the brief describes. All 12 pairs below are therefore drawn from **bf24 alone**; nothing here generalises across foods, which is reflected in the VERDICT.

Measurement conventions used below (operator, per docs/CAPTURE_PROTOCOL.md §5): **syringe** = 10 ml Luer slip-tip, vertical, nozzle 25 mm above bench, read ml remaining at t=10 s, L3 acceptance 8–10 ml; **spoon-tilt** = 15 g on a 20 ml spoon tilted to 45° in 1 s, record time for the leading edge to travel 10 mm and peak height at 30 s; **fork-drip** = 15 g on a 4-tine dinner fork held horizontal 100 mm above bench, record ribbon formation and drops/30 s; **fork-pressure** = peak force on a 0.1 N scale under the piece, plus whether it splits into identifiable fragments; **particle check** = calipers on 20 random particles from a 50 g spread, report max and p90.

CONTRASTS:

  pair 1: food=bf24 level=L3/L4
    A: puree braised melon 90 s, pass through a 1 mm chinois, reduce liquor to 12 °Brix, **no hydrocolloid** — the continuous phase is thinned only by its own free water. looks: 60 g quenelle, pale jade, high gloss, lump-free, same white plate and exposure, no bubbles, no visible structure. measures: syringe → **9.1 ml remaining at t=10 s** (inside the L3 window); spoon-tilt 45° → leading edge 10 mm in 2.5 s, peak 0 mm at 30 s; fork-drip → continuous ribbon in 4 s, 21 drops/30 s.
    B: identical puree, identical reduction, **plus 0.16 % xanthan** blended 60 s and rested 10 min. looks: the same 60 g quenelle, same jade, same gloss — 0.16 % xanthan is a clear gel: no matte cast, no cloudiness, no speckle; at 1:1 framing the two frames are indistinguishable. measures: syringe → **10.0 ml remaining** (no flow; outside the L3 window); spoon-tilt 45° → peak 15 mm held >30 s, no measurable travel; fork-drip → 0 drops/30 s; fork-pressure → 2.1 N through, no discrete failure (L4 lump-free).
    WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: the only change is 0.06 % of a clear, colourless, tasteless hydrocolloid, and it moves the sample 0.9 ml in a reading no camera can make.
    STRENGTH: strong — the L3/L4 viscosity boundary isolated to a single invisible variable, both sides inside their own level's acceptance window.

  pair 2: food=bf24 level=L3/L4

    B: the **same 200 g batch**, held 4 h at 4 °C, tested at 6 °C. looks: the same quenelle, same gloss, same jade — a chilled glossy puree and a warm one photograph identically once the plate is wiped; nothing in frame records temperature. measures: syringe → **10.0 ml remaining**; spoon-tilt 45° → peak 12 mm held >30 s (holds a soft shape → L4); fork-pressure → 2.4 N, no discrete failure.
    WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: it is literally the same food at the same framing at two temperatures — the level flips and the image does not change at all.
    STRENGTH: strong — same batch, operator and camera, opposite level; the cleanest available demonstration that the defining variable is absent from the frame.

  pair 3: food=bf24 level=L3/L4
    A: identical ingredients and batch weight, **blended 30 s at low speed** — coarse dispersion, cell walls largely intact. looks: 60 g quenelle, jade, satin sheen, lump-free to the eye. measures: syringe → **8.9 ml remaining at t=10 s** (L3 window); spoon-tilt 45° → leading edge 10 mm in 3.1 s, peak 0 mm at 30 s; particle check → 0 particles >1 mm.

    WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: same recipe, same mass, same colour, same lump-free status — the only difference is how long the blades ran.

  pair 4: food=bf24 level=L4/L5
    A: L4 base (puree reduced to 14 °Brix, 0.15 % xanthan), **no added particles**. looks: 70 g mound, glossy, uniformly jade, spoon-swept to a smooth plain with one clean ridge, no visible inclusions. measures: spoon-tilt 45° → peak 16 mm held >30 s; fork-pressure → 1.9 N through, no discrete failure; particle check on 50 g → 0 particles >1 mm.

    WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: the inclusions are the same food as the matrix, cooked in the same liquor, so colour and sheen are matched by construction — only the particle check separates the two.
    STRENGTH: strong — the L4/L5 boundary is an absolute particle-size boundary, and base viscosity, colour, gloss, plating and mass are all held constant.

  pair 5: food=bf24 level=L4/L5

    WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: same pot, sampled twice, colour matched back — the only difference is whether the dice survived the braise.
    STRENGTH: strong — same batch, operator and liquor; the level changes because structure collapsed, which is exactly the information a single frame cannot carry.

  pair 6: food=bf24 level=L5/L6

REPEATS:
  bf24_braised_winter_melon_L3 x3 — the defining property is the continuous phase; reduction time and blend duration both move the syringe reading across a window only 2 ml wide (8–10 ml), so this is where preparation noise most easily flips the level.
  bf24_braised_winter_melon_L3 x2 — same operator, second day, second melon: winter melon is ~96 % water, so lot-to-lot water content is the dominant noise source for a flowing puree and is invisible in a photo.
  bf24_braised_winter_melon_L4 x3 — "holds a soft shape" is the most subjective reading in the set; three independent preps give the spread of spoon-tilt peak height and slump time that separates a real L4 from an L3.
  bf24_braised_winter_melon_L4 x2 — hydrocolloid dosing repeatability: sub-0.5 % w/w weighing error is the difference between 9.5 ml and 10.0 ml remaining, i.e. L3 vs L4.
  bf24_braised_winter_melon_L5 x3 — knife-cut accuracy around the 4 mm target; ±1 mm of cut error straddles an absolute boundary, so this measures cut variance directly.
  bf24_braised_winter_melon_L5 x2 — braising-time repeatability at fixed dice size: overrun collapses the dice and turns a genuine L5 into something that photographs like L4.
  bf24_braised_winter_melon_L6 x2 — the 8–15 mm band is 7 mm wide; the repeat shows where in the band the operator actually lands and whether that choice is stable across preps.
  bf24_braised_winter_melon_L6 x2 — fork-pressure force at fixed 9 mm size, three presses per prep, separating tine-placement error from genuine preparation variance in firmness.
  bf24_braised_winter_melon_L7 x2 — the ≥15 mm floor is a hard cut; this counts how often an intended L7 dice comes out at 13–14 mm and has to be reclassified as L6.
  bf24_braised_winter_melon_L7 x2 — whether "needs chewing" is reached by size alone or by an unintended firmness increase; pairs the force reading with the size reading so the two stay decoupled.
  bf24_braised_winter_melon_L5 x2 — second day, second lot: moisture at the "moist, fork-mashable" call — dry-side vs wet-side preps of the identical recipe.
  bf24_braised_winter_melon_L6 x2 — second lot: the fork-force reading for the same nominal braise time on melon of different maturity, i.e. the noise floor for the L6/L7 boundary.

WEAKEST_CONTRAST: pair 9 — 14 mm vs 20 mm is a 43 % linear and 2× area difference, single-layer framing actively helps the viewer, and the pair will most likely be "won" for the trivial reason that the cubes really are visibly different; it therefore contributes least to the question. Runner-up is pair 7, where a flake's outline is a strong "not a cube" cue that partly defeats the shape confound.

VERDICT: INSUFFICIENT — (1) d10's sealed set exposes 5 specimens from one food, not the 12 × 5 the brief describes, so no pair here generalises across the set; (2) 12 contrasts at n = 1 per side cannot be scored until the repeats establish a noise floor — a pair is only evidence if the measured separation between A and B exceeds the repeat spread for that level, and that threshold does not exist yet; (3) the design is nonetheless capable of deciding the question within bf24, since pairs 2, 5, 8, 10, 11 and 12 are decisive in form, so the verdict is deferred rather than negative.
