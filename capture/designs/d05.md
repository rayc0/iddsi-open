I have the sealed set (bf10 — steamed pumpkin, L3–L7) and the capture protocol. Here is the design.

CONTRASTS: (all pairs drawn from bf10_steamed_pumpkin, the only base food in this batch's sealed set; A = on-target preparation of the declared specimen, B = visually matched near-miss that tests an adjacent level; gum/starch doses are titrated in 0.02% steps against the target test reading, using one pumpkin lot for both sides)

pair 1: food=bf10 level=L4
  A: Blended steamed Kent pumpkin with 20% of its steaming liquid, 1 mm sieve pass, rested 10 min at 20 °C, guar titrated to the L4 target. looks: deep-orange, uniformly glossy, dead-flat levelled surface at the same bowl fill mark, no lumps, no utensil marks, 15 mm card in frame, same white bowl/light/plate as B. measures: spoon-tilt at 45° — no flow in 10 s, holds a soft peak; fork-drip — sits on the tines as one mass, ≤1 mL exudes past the slots at 10 s; fork-press — mass deforms under light pressure, no rebound.
  B: Identical batch and plating with the guar dose reduced to the L3 target. looks: identical colour, fill mark and flat levelled mirror surface, same bowl and lighting, no lumps; a sub-0.3% gum change is not resolvable in colour or gloss at 45°. measures: spoon-tilt — flows off the spoon over 8–10 s leaving a thin film; fork-drip — continuous stream past the slots within 8 s, fork clear at 10 s; syringe flow (run on B because its tested level is L3 — record as a protocol deviation note) — 8.5–9 mL residual at t=10 s, inside the 8–10 mL L3 window.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: two lump-free, identically plated glossy orange purees; the entire difference is how slowly each moves under gravity, which a static 45° still cannot encode.
  STRENGTH: strong — this is the L3/L4 viscosity boundary itself with A and B astride it; the only still cue is specular gloss, and solids are matched closely enough that neither assessor can rank the pair from the still.

pair 2: food=bf10 level=L3
  A: Same pumpkin base thickened with ~1.5% w/w cornstarch, cooked 3 min, cooled 20 min — shear-thinning, negligible yield stress. looks: deep-orange, glossy, same fill, flat levelled surface, no lumps, same bowl/light as B. measures: 10 mL syringe — 9 ± 0.5 mL residual at t=10 s (inside the L3 window); spoon-tilt — flows off in ~5 s; fork-drip — streams through the slots in ~3 s.
  B: Same base thickened with ~0.30% guar — high low-shear viscosity. looks: identical colour, fill and levelled surface; gum is marginally glossier, mitigated by matching total solids and shooting both in one session at one angle. measures: syringe — 8.5 ± 0.5 mL residual at t=10 s (also inside the L3 window); spoon-tilt — no flow in 10 s, holds a soft peak; fork-drip — sits on the tines as one mass.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: both pass the applicable L3 syringe test and are indistinguishable in colour, sheen and lump-freeness; they differ only in response at low shear.
  STRENGTH: strong — a within-level instrument discordance: one photograph supports L3 (A on tilt/drip) and L4 (B on tilt/drip) while both read L3 on the syringe, so the tested level depends on which applicable test is trusted.

pair 3: food=bf10 level=L3
  A: Pumpkin puree set with ~0.5% low-methoxyl pectin + ~0.15% calcium lactate — a weak yield-stress gel. looks: deep-orange, glossy, same fill, flat levelled surface, no lumps, same bowl/light as B. measures: syringe — 9 ± 0.5 mL residual at t=10 s (inside L3); spoon-tilt — no motion for 8 s, then slumps off as a single block; fork-drip — bridges the slots, ≤1 mL passes at 10 s.
  B: Same base with ~0.30% guar, no gel — matched syringe reading, no yield stress. looks: identical colour, fill, levelled surface, no lumps. measures: syringe — 9 ± 0.5 mL residual at t=10 s; spoon-tilt — creeps continuously off in ~8 s; fork-drip — streams through the slots within 5 s.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: matched on the syringe test, colour, sheen and lump-freeness; the difference is yield stress versus viscosity, visible only in the shape of the motion.
  STRENGTH: strong — separates the two properties the L3/L4 boundary conflates; both sides pass L3 on the syringe, so a still gives no basis for choosing, and the pair tests directly whether "holds a soft shape" is photographable at all.

pair 4: food=bf10 level=L4
  A: One L4 batch (pair 1A recipe) served at 62 °C — heat-thinned. looks: deep-orange glossy puree, same bowl and fill as B, photographed in the same 30 s post-plating window. measures: spoon-tilt — flows off in ~4 s; fork-drip — streams past the slots in ~3 s; syringe loaded at 62 °C — 8.5 ± 0.5 mL residual at t=10 s (tests L3).
  B: The same single batch, split and chilled to 12 °C — cold-thickened. looks: identical colour and fill; the only cues are faint steam above A and condensation on A's bowl rim in the first ~20 s. measures: spoon-tilt — no flow in 10 s, holds a soft peak; fork-drip — sits on the tines; syringe — 10 mL residual at t=10 s (tests L4).
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: it is literally the same cooked batch, so colour, gloss, lump-freeness and plating are identical by construction.
  STRENGTH: moderate — a clean single-variable manipulation of the commonest real-world confound, but steam/condensation and the sheen shift give a visible cue, and serving temperature is a recorded manifest field, so this is a labelled covariate rather than withheld information.

pair 5: food=bf10 level=L4
  A: L4 puree thickened with ~2.4% cornstarch — tacky, high adhesion. looks: identical glossy orange puree, same bowl, fill and levelled surface as B, no lumps. measures: spoon-tilt clip — after release the spoon bowl keeps a continuous coating over ~100% of its surface; fork-drip clip — tines stay loaded, material drapes rather than separates; spoon-tilt flow and fork-drip passage both matched to B (holds a soft peak, sits on the tines).
  B: Same base with ~0.35% guar plus ~1.5% maltodextrin to match solids and viscosity — non-tacky, releases cleanly. looks: identical colour, fill, levelled surface, no lumps; gloss matched by titrating maltodextrin/water. measures: spoon-tilt clip — ≤10% residue on the spoon bowl after release; fork-drip clip — clean release from the tines.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: both are levelled, lump-free, identically glossy orange purees; adhesion appears only on the utensil surface after release, which the required 45° still never shows.
  STRENGTH: moderate — the reading is real and is exactly the "utensil surface after release" the protocol requires to stay visible, but adhesiveness is not part of the L3/L4 definition, so this probes what a still cannot show about mouthfeel rather than the level boundary itself.

pair 6: food=bf10 level=L5
  A: Firm-fleshed pumpkin hand-cut and mashed to a 3.0 mm maximum (3 mm sieve) folded through a fine mash base — tests L4. looks: moist, finely speckled deep-orange mash, same fill and mound height as B, no visible free liquid, 15 mm card in frame. measures: IDDSI particle-size check against the card — largest piece 3.0 mm, nothing ≥4 mm (L4 by test); spoon-tilt — mash slides and slumps with no discrete lumps to press; fork-press — nothing to blanch, mass deforms uniformly.
  B: Same pumpkin and mash base with 4.5 mm dice folded through — on-target L5. looks: also reads as "finely speckled moist mash" at 45°; at 4.5 mm the lumps are ~30% of the card width and present as the same speckle, no free liquid, same mound height. measures: particle-size check — largest pieces 4.5 mm, inside the L5 ~4 mm band; spoon-tilt — discrete lumps visible in the scoop; fork-press — each lump deforms to thumbnail blanching under light pressure, no rebound.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: both are moist orange mashes of the same colour, sheen and mound; the whole difference is 1.5 mm of particle size, right at the visibility threshold in 45° overhead framing.
  STRENGTH: strong — this is the L4/L5 absolute particle-size boundary, the study's second key hypothesis; it is also the pair most likely to be genuinely visible because the 15 mm card supplies scale, so it doubles as a positive control proving the apparatus can detect a real difference.

pair 7: food=bf10 level=L5
  A: 4.5 mm dice steamed 22 min, very tender — on-target fork-mashable L5. looks: moist 4.5 mm dice in a fine mash base, same colour, same surface sheen, no free liquid, same mound as B. measures: fork-press to thumbnail blanching — each lump flattens under light pressure, full deformation, no rebound; spoon-tilt — lumps slide with the mash.
  B: Same 4.5 mm dice steamed 12 min, firm — tests L6 behaviour. looks: identical 4.5 mm dice, same colour and mound; the only cue is a fractionally drier, more matte surface, mitigated by a 10 s blot on both sides and matching the mash-base gloss. measures: fork-press — resists light pressure, needs sustained pressure to blanch the thumbnail, partial rebound, piece stays intact.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: identical particle size, colour and plating; the difference is parenchyma firmness, which leaves no trace in a still.
  STRENGTH: moderate — real and level-relevant, but the fork-press endpoint is a visual "thumbnail blanching" endpoint rather than a force reading, and the two steaming times do carry a small (if mitigated) sheen difference.

pair 8: food=bf10 level=L5
  A: 4.5 mm mash bound with ~0.2% guar — cohesive. looks: levelled moist orange mash with identical 4.5 mm speckle, same colour, sheen and mound as B. measures: spoon-tilt — the mass moves as one piece and holds a mound on the spoon; fork-press — a pressed lump holds together without crumbling.
  B: Same 4.5 mm particles, unbound, slightly drier mash base — friable. looks: identical speckle, colour and levelled surface when plated flat, no visible free liquid. measures: spoon-tilt — the mass fractures, particles separate and flow around one another; fork-press — the lump crumbles before blanching.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: plated flat and levelled, both read as the same speckled orange mound; cohesiveness only appears once a utensil disturbs the mass.
  STRENGTH: moderate — observable in the applicable spoon-tilt and fork-press clips, but "crumb vs hold" is a category rather than a number, and any non-levelled plating would leak the difference straight into the still, so plating must be controlled.

pair 9: food=bf10 level=L6
  A: 10 mm dice steamed 22 min, very tender — tests L5 behaviour at L6 size. looks: pale-orange 10 mm cubes in a fine moist base, no free liquid, same plate and fill as B, 15 mm card in frame. measures: fork-press to thumbnail blanching — cube compresses fully under light pressure, deforms to blanching, no rebound (L5-like); particle size 10 mm.
  B: Same 10 mm dice steamed 13 min — on-target L6. looks: identical 10 mm cubes, same colour, same moist sheen, no free liquid. measures: fork-press — compresses to thumbnail blanching with moderate pressure, slight rebound, piece holds its shape; particle size 10 mm, inside the 8–15 mm L6 band.

  STRENGTH: strong — size is fixed at 10 mm so the fork-press outcome is the only discriminator, which isolates the firmness axis from the size axis of the L5/L6 boundary; the caveat is that the endpoint is qualitative, so RD and SLP must record it independently with third-party adjudication.

pair 11: food=bf10 level=L7
  A: 18 mm regular dice steamed 13 min — on-target L7. looks: regular pale-orange 18 mm cubes, moist, no free liquid, same plate and fill as B, 15 mm card in frame. measures: fork-press to thumbnail blanching — cube cannot be compressed to blanching without fracturing, rebounds, requires chewing (L7); particle size 18 mm, ≥15 mm, regular.

pair 12: food=bf10 level=L7
  A: 16 mm dice on a 30 cm plate photographed at 55 cm — pieces subtend a small angle. looks: pale-orange 16 mm cubes that look small in the frame, 15 mm card in frame. measures: particle size against the card — 16 mm, ≥15 mm (L7); fork-press — requires chewing.
  B: 13 mm dice on a 16 cm plate photographed at 30 cm — pieces subtend the same angle as A. looks: the same apparent cube size in the frame, same colour, moisture and plating; only the card and the plate edge reveal the difference. measures: particle size — 13 mm, inside the 8–15 mm L6 band (L6); fork-press — soft, cuttable.
  WHY_HARD_TO_DISTINGUISH: by construction the two sides have the same apparent piece size in frame; only the 15 mm card disambiguates.
  STRENGTH: moderate — it varies capture framing rather than food physics, and because the card is in frame the information is in principle recoverable, so a failure here diagnoses card/scale handling rather than missing photographic content; the differing plate diameters also add a capture confound (though the protocol mandates ≥3 plate appearances).

REPEATS: each repeat is a new batch_id and new event_id prepared by the same operator with plate position, lighting category and serving temperature held at the first batch's values, and with the operator blind to the first batch's measured readings; re-shooting one batch is a retake, not a repeat. Same-operator repeats cannot estimate between-operator variance, so the noise floor below is a lower bound.
  bf10_steamed_pumpkin_L3 x 3 — blend time, sieve passes and added-liquid drift; exposes the spread in syringe 10 s residual and spoon-tilt flow time right at the 8–10 mL L3 window — the noise floor that decides whether pairs 1–3 are interpretable.
  bf10_steamed_pumpkin_L4 x 3 — thickener weighing (±0.05 g at a 0.30% dose) and rest/cooling time; exposes whether an independently prepared L4 lands on the same side of "holds a soft shape" — if repeats straddle that line, pairs 1, 2, 4 and 5 are uninterpretable.
  bf10_steamed_pumpkin_L5 x 2 — hand-dicing and mashing scatter; exposes how many lumps fall above the 4 mm threshold, which on an absolute boundary is a level change rather than noise (pairs 6–8).
  bf10_steamed_pumpkin_L6 x 2 — steaming-time and dice-size scatter within the 8–15 mm band; exposes the spread in fork-press deformation/rebound and in measured piece size (pairs 9–10).
  bf10_steamed_pumpkin_L7 x 2 — piece-size and doneness scatter at ≥15 mm; exposes whether "needs chewing" reproduces and how far the fork-press outcome moves (pairs 11–12).

WEAKEST_CONTRAST: pair 4 — the 62 °C side shows steam and bowl condensation for the first ~20 s and carries a slightly different surface sheen, so the pair does not actually satisfy "look almost identical"; and because serving temperature is recorded on the manifest, the difference is a labelled covariate rather than information the photograph withholds.

VERDICT: INSUFFICIENT — no specimen in this batch has been physically tested, so there is no photograph-to-measurement comparison to score; the design is adequate to adjudicate once run: a pair counts as evidence only if A and B test on opposite sides of a level boundary and that specimen's repeat noise floor is below one level, and the claim is SUPPORTED if the model assigns A and B the same predicted level in ≥8 such pairs (necessarily wrong on one side) and REFUTED if it separates them in ≥10.
