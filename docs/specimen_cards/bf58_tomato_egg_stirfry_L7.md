**BF58 · tomato egg stir-fry 番茄炒蛋 · target L7 (whole/regular: pieces ≥15 mm, regular, needs chewing)**
**Bought form:** homemade or takeaway. **Allergens:** egg — confirm against the recipe or pack label at portioning and log extras in `prep.notes` (some recipes add soy sauce or MSG).
**Tests that apply at L7:** fork pressure (5.1) only.

1. PREP
- Weigh **200 g** of tomato egg stir-fry as served, straight from the container onto the tray, and record the mass.
- **No blending, no sieving, no chopping, no thickener, no added liquid, no pressing and no folding.** L7 is the food as bought and normally served; each of those steps moves the specimen toward L6/L5/L4.
- Plate as bought, at serving temperature, and capture within **10 min** of plating. Do not chill the portion — cold egg sets firmer and reads harder than the dish as it is normally eaten.
- Piece-size check: hold two or three pieces (one tomato wedge and one egg curd) against the 100 mm ruler. Every piece must measure **at least 15 mm** on its longest dimension and must still need chewing. If the batch is served finely scrambled, L7 is not reachable for it — see FAIL MODES.

2. IDDSI TEST
- Fork pressure (5.1 — the only test that applies at L7): place one piece on the tray, press the flat of the fork bowl down onto it with the thumb until the thumbnail blanches white, then lift. **Pass** = the piece compresses under that pressure and does not recover its shape when the fork is lifted; it stays a distinct piece the fork lifts whole; no free red liquid runs from it.
- Sizing check, in the same unbroken take: hold two or three pieces against the 100 mm ruler. Every piece must be **≥15 mm** on its longest dimension.
- Spoon tilt: **skip** — 5.2 covers L3/L4/L5. L7 is not a spoonable mass, so this test is not run and is not recorded as pass/fail.
- Fork drip: **skip** — 5.3 covers L3/L4. There is no flow through the tines to observe on a solid piece, so this test is not run and is not recorded as pass/fail.
- Syringe flow: **skip** — 5.4 is required on every L3 only; a solid piece is not a 10 ml flow sample.

3. VISUAL QC
- Distinct tomato wedges and egg curds, each ≥15 mm, individually separable in the tray.
- Red tomato juice and egg coat the pieces and the tray but do not pool into a continuous separate liquid layer.
- Pieces hold their shape when nudged with the fork tines; the fork leaves a compression mark that does not spring back.
- No oil layer spreading across the tray.

5. FAIL MODES
- Pieces measure **under 15 mm** on their longest dimension (a finely scrambled or over-stirred batch): no bench step increases piece size. Record the measured size, mark the row FAIL, and re-source a chunkier batch — tomato wedges of about 20–30 mm with curds of the same order.
- Pieces smear to paste under blanching pressure (**too soft — below L7, drifting toward L5/L6**): the batch is over-cooked or over-stirred. Re-source a batch with intact wedges; no bench step firms a stir-fry, and chilling only stiffens the oil.
- Egg curds collapse to fragments during handling: re-plate from an untouched part of the container without pressing.
- Free red juice or oil pooling into a continuous layer so the pieces sit in liquid: re-plate from a freshly served portion, wipe the tray edge, re-shoot within 60 s. If the batch is inherently watery, record FAIL and re-source.
- Achievability, stated plainly: this dish is normally served in ≥15 mm wedges, so L7 is reachable only from a bought form cut that way. A takeaway that serves it finely chopped cannot be labelled L7 however it is handled — this is the failure the previous empty card invited, because a soft, moist stir-fry handled carelessly lands at L5–L6 and still gets labelled L7.
- **Never blend, sieve, chop or thicken an L7 specimen** — those are the L4/L3 steps and would produce a mislabelled L7 row.
