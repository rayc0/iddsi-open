PREP
Weigh 150 g peeled ripe banana on the digital scale and record the mass. Mash thoroughly with a fork against the bowl wall for 60-90 seconds until uniform; do not use the blender — blending collapses the loose L5 particle structure into the smooth puree that L4 requires. Gauge the mash with the 4 mm sieve: rub one fork-load at a time over the mesh, re-mash anything held back, and repeat until the whole portion passes, so that no particle exceeds 4 mm. The sieve is a particle gauge, not a finishing step — the finished specimen must still read as loose soft moist particles and never as a smooth satin puree. Fold briefly to redistribute moisture, then confirm and record the final mass on the scale. Add no thickener: 0 g at this level. Thickener is the L4 calibration starting point (banana needs roughly 1.5 g per 100 g of puree before it will hold a shape), so a specimen that only behaves once thickener goes in is L4 and must be filed against the L4 card. Add no liquid to begin with — ripe banana is moist as-is. Only a mash that is dry and crumbly and will not hold a mound is moistened, with water in 1 ml steps per 100 g, re-testing after every step.

IDDSI TEST
Applicable tests at L5, per docs/CAPTURE_PROTOCOL.md section 5: the 4 mm sieve gauge, the spoon-tilt test, and the fork-pressure test as a supporting check. The fork-drip test and the 10 ml / 10 s syringe flow test are L3/L4 tests: they do not apply at L5 and must not be run, filmed or recorded as L5 evidence.

4 mm sieve gauge: rub one fork-load of mash over the mesh; re-mash and re-pass anything retained until the whole portion passes. Pass = nothing is retained above 4 mm.

Spoon tilt test: scoop a level teaspoon, hold it level, then tilt it sideways to 90 degrees over the bowl. Pass at L5 = the soft moist particles hold a loose mound that slumps and spreads as it comes off the spoon; the spoon is not swimming in separate liquid; the specimen does not pour off in a stream. Record on camera that no separate thin liquid pools on the spoon or the tray — any pooling means the specimen fails its level.

Fork pressure test (supporting check only): press the tines of the fork into a heaped spoonful until the thumbnail blanches white, then withdraw. Pass = the mash squashes readily and does not recover its shape when the fork is lifted.

Boundary against L4: a specimen that holds its shape on the spoon and slides off as one cohesive mass with little residue left behind, that sits in a mound above the fork tines with no drip at all, or that takes a clean indentation is behaving as L4 puree, not L5. It has not passed L5, whatever the sieve gauge says — re-label it and shoot the L4 test set (fork pressure, spoon tilt, fork drip) instead.

VISUAL QC
Loose soft moist particles, not a puree: the mound on the spoon has an open, grainy surface with shoulders that slump when the spoon is tilted, and it carries a slight natural sheen rather than the satin gloss of a blended puree. No particle above 4 mm against the ruler or the checkerboard. Colour uniform pale yellow with no white fibrous threads. No separate thin liquid pooling on the spoon, the bowl or the tray. No held shape, no clean-cut edges and no gel-like gloss — those are L4 and L6 features and disqualify the specimen at L5.

FAIL MODES
(1) Particles or fibrous strands above 4 mm — re-mash and re-pass through the 4 mm sieve until the whole portion passes. (2) The specimen reads as a smooth puree, holds a clean shape, or slides off the spoon as one cohesive mass — that is L4 behaviour; add no thickener to force a pass. Re-label and shoot the L4 test set (fork pressure, spoon tilt, fork drip). (3) Thin liquid pools or separates — the specimen fails its level. Do NOT drain it on a paper towel: draining strips out the moisture that makes this L5 and leaves a denser, puree-like mass that then passes an L4-style fork-drip check. Re-make from a reserve aliquot with a just-ripe banana; if it pools again, record the event as FAIL with tests.pass = false. (4) The mash is dry and crumbly and will not hold a mound, or it recovers its shape after the fork press — moisten with water in 1 ml steps per 100 g, re-testing after each step; if it then pours off the spoon in a stream it has gone too far and is L3, so re-make. (5) The banana is underripe and the mash stays firm and grainy — use a riper fruit. Record any remake in the log.
