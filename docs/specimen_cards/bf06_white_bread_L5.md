1. PREP
Start with 30 g of crust-off white bread torn into 1 cm pieces. Place in consumer blender. Add 90 ml water (1:3 bread-to-water). Blend on high for 25 s. Pass through the 4 mm sieve using a fork to push soft pulp through; discard any coarse residue retained on mesh — if more than ~20 % of the blend stays on the sieve, the bread is too fibrous to reach L5 as a single specimen and the operator should note this and switch to a fork-mash method instead: place 30 g torn bread in a bowl, add 30 ml water, mash with fork for 60 s until a uniform coarse paste forms, then sieve to confirm ≤4 mm. L5 does not require thickener; calibration entry is "0 g/100 ml" and the operator records actual as 0.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork pressure: press fork tines into a heaped teaspoon of specimen on a flat plate until thumbnail blanches white. Specimen must squash completely and not spring back. Spoon tilt: tilt a spoonful 45°; specimen must hold its shape with no drip or run. Fork drip: lift fork loaded with specimen; nothing must drip through tines or off within 10 s. (Syringe flow test not required at L5.)

3. VISUAL QC
Surface looks matte and damp, never glossy or pooling. Particles on the fork are soft lumps, not crumbs, all clearly under 4 mm. A heaped teaspoon holds its mound for ≥5 s without slumping.

4. SHOTS
Capture three stills: (a) 45° hero with ruler along left edge and checkerboard top-left, specimen on white plate; (b) top-down, checkerboard filling frame, specimen centred; (c) fork-press close-up mid-press, tines half-submerged. Capture two clips: (d) 8–12 s fork-press + spoon-tilt sequence; (e) IDDSI test film, fork-pressure and spoon-tilt continuous. 5. FAIL MODES
Specimen is too wet: liquid pools on plate or drips from fork — return to Step 1, reduce water by 10 ml. Specimen is too dry: particles hold shape and spring back from fork pressure — add 5 ml water and re-mash. Visible lumps >4 mm after sieving: re-blend 10 s and re-sieve. Re-make from fresh bread after two failed attempts.
