1. PREP
Weigh 100 g rolled oats into the blender. Add 200 ml hot water. Blend 45-60 s on high until visually smooth with no intact flakes. Sieve a 30 g portion through the 4 mm sieve — at L4 target, zero grains should pass and the mass on the sieve should hold as a cohesive mound; if grains do pass, blend an extra 15 s and re-test. No thickener needed for L4; record 0 g/100 ml as the calibration starting point. No additional water or milk.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork drip test: lift a forkful and hold it above the bowl for 5 s — the specimen must sit in a mound above the tines with zero drip falling through. Pass = no drip. Fork pressure test: press the fork tines into the specimen on a flat plate with thumb pressure — the surface must blanch white but the tines must not cut cleanly through; pass = blanch without full separation. Spoon tilt test: scoop the specimen, then tilt the spoon 45° — the mass must not slide, pour, or fall off; pass = holds shape with no flow. No separate thin liquid must pool at the rim of the specimen on the plate.

3. VISUAL QC
Surface is uniform cream/beige with no visible oat flecks or bubbles. Coats the spoon back evenly with no run-back. Holds a peaked mound on the fork tines with clean edges.

5. FAIL MODES
Drips through fork tines or slumps off the tilted spoon → too thin; re-blend 15 s on high and re-sieve, no extra water. Visible oat flecks or grains pass the sieve → under-blended; re-blend 30 s and re-sieve. White liquid pools at the plate rim → syneresis; discard and re-make with a fresh batch using the same 100 g oats : 200 ml water ratio.
