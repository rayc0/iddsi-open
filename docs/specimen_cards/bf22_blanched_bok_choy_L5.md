PREP
Weigh 200 g blanched bok choy on the scale. Pat dry briefly with paper towel, then chop finely with a chef's knife on a board, aiming for pieces under 4 mm in every dimension. The per-level note permits water moistening; add 20 ml warm water and mash with a fork until the mass is cohesive, no separate thin liquid pools on the board, and no piece exceeds 4 mm when checked against the 4 mm sieve. Sieve a small scoop to confirm; if any fragment sits on top of the mesh, rec chop that portion and re-mash. Do not blend — the per-level note calls for fork-mash, not puree, and a consumer blender would over-process this leaf into a pourable fluid. No thickener; L5 does not calibrate thickener, so do not add any.

IDDSI TEST
Fork-pressure test: press the flat of the fork into a heaped teaspoon of specimen with firm thumb pressure until the thumbnail blanches white. The specimen must squash, the fork tines leave a clear imprint, and the food must NOT spring back when the fork is lifted. Spoon-tilt test: tilt a teaspoon 45 degrees; the specimen should hold together as one mass and slide off slowly in a single clump, not flow, drip, or run. Fork-drip test: lift a loaded fork; no thin liquid should drip through the tines within 10 seconds.

VISUAL QC
On the fork: a coarse, granular mash with visible green flecks, glistening with moisture but not glossy or soupy. On the spoon: holds a peaked mound without slumping; no standing liquid in the spoon bowl. Color stays bok-choy green-white, not pale yellow-green from over-blanching.

FAIL MODES
Specimen recovers shape after fork press → too dry or chopped too coarse; re-mash with 5 ml water per 100 g and re-check against the 4 mm sieve. Thin liquid pools on the board or drips off the fork → over-moistened; drain on paper towel and re-mash. Pieces exceed 4 mm → rec chop those fragments, do not blend.
