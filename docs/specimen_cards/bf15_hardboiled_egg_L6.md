1. PREP
Weigh 200 g peeled hard-boiled egg (white and yolk). Before chopping, mark an 8 mm line and a 15 mm line on the board using the ruler and a marker. Chop on the board with a knife into pieces whose longest dimension measures 8–15 mm — no piece under 8 mm and none over 15 mm. Check the longest edge of every piece against the two marked lines. Do not blend — the level note says chop, not puree. Do not sieve. Do not add water or thickener. Record the actual mass of the chopped specimen in g.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork pressure (primary): press the fork tines onto a piece until the thumbnail blanches white. Pass — the piece squashes and does not return to shape; it breaks apart, not springs back.
Spoon tilt: skip — L6 is not spoonable, so tilt is not the deciding test.
Fork drip: skip — L6 pieces do not drip.
Run fork-pressure as the primary test.

3. VISUAL QC
Pieces are irregular, soft-looking, yellow-cream, with visible yolk and white mixed. Hold a piece on the fork — it visibly deforms under its own weight and when pressed. No liquid weeps from the piece. Check every piece against the 100 mm ruler: each must measure 8–15 mm on its longest edge. A batch containing pieces under 8 mm is not L6 material — see FAIL MODES.

5. FAIL MODES
Pieces over 15 mm — re-chop.
Pieces under 8 mm — the batch is too fine for L6 (sub-8 mm pieces are L5 material and must not be captured and labelled L6): discard the undersize pieces and re-chop the remainder to size; if more than about 1 piece in 10 is under 8 mm, start again with a fresh egg and chop coarser.
Egg rubbery and springs back under fork — egg overcooked, start again with a freshly boiled egg.
Pieces crumble to powder rather than squash — egg undercooked, re-boil.
