1. PREP
Start with 200 g bitter melon flesh, seeds and pith removed. Steam 20 min, then check every 2 min to a hard cap of 25 min total. The batch is ready only when a piece pressed with the fork tines until the thumbnail blanches white squashes and does not recover its shape — the section 5.1 criterion, not a thumbnail mark: a piece can leave a clean, persistent white mark under the thumbnail and still spring back, which is the L7 direction and must not be read as "done". Cool 60 s. Slice into pieces 8–15 mm in every dimension, measuring each against the 100 mm ruler: no piece may be under 8 mm or over 15 mm. Re-cut any piece over 15 mm. Fines under 8 mm are set aside and never plated, never photographed and never certified as L6 — a sub-8 mm fine in frame is L5 material carrying an L6 label, and the photograph is the training input. Re-weigh the plated portion and confirm every piece on the plate reads 8–15 mm before any shot is taken. Do not blend and do not sieve. No thickener, no added water or liquid. The pieces must hold their own shape when lifted on the fork, and must squash without recovery when pressed.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork-pressure test (the only L6 test): press the fork tines onto one 8–15 mm piece on a flat plate, chosen without selecting it for softness, until the thumbnail blanches white. Pass criterion: the piece squashes under the tines, does not recover its shape when the fork is lifted, AND remains a single coherent piece that can be lifted clear on the tines. A piece that breaks apart, smears into purée or releases free water FAILS — that is the L5 direction, and "it squashed and did not recover" is not on its own an L6 pass. Repeat on three further pieces from different parts of the batch; all three must pass and a failing piece is not swapped for an easier one. Confirm with the ruler that each tested piece measures 8–15 mm (not under 8 mm, not over 15 mm).
Spoon tilt: not applicable for L6.
Fork drip: not applicable for L6.
Syringe flow: not applicable for L6.
L6 is confirmed by fork-pressure alone plus the 8–15 mm ruler check.

3. VISUAL QC
Pieces on fork should look moist but not wet, with visible ridges from the melon interior intact. Edges are soft and rounded, not torn or fibrous. Colour is translucent olive-green; no free liquid pools on the plate. A fork press with the thumbnail blanching white leaves the piece visibly squashed, with no spring-back and no purée smear at the tines.

5. FAIL MODES
Pieces larger than 15 mm: re-cut smaller, do not re-steam. Pieces under 8 mm: re-cut from a larger piece; do not certify sub-8 mm fines as L6 and do not leave them on the plate — a plate carrying fines is a mixed-size plate and the hero shot is a mislabelled L6 image.
Pieces rubbery or springing back under the fork (too firm, L7-like): re-steam in 2 min steps and re-test after each, but never past the 25 min total cap in the PREP. If the flesh is still rubbery and springs back at 25 min, it cannot reach L6 softness by this bench method: the specimen behaves as L7 (firm, needs chewing) and must NOT be certified L6. Re-make once from the reserve aliquot or mark the event FAIL with tests.pass = false and the reason in prep.notes — the media is never renamed into L7 (protocol section 7, QC rule 1); the L7 label belongs to the L7 aliquot, which is whole steamed rings served as-is.
Pieces breaking to puree, smearing, or releasing free water under the fork (too soft, L5 direction): this fails the fork-pressure pass criterion above, not just the look of the plate. Discard the batch and reduce steam time on the next batch. Do not try to rescue it by re-cutting smaller, which only moves the specimen further toward L5.
Reliability: bitter melon flesh steamed on its own tends to stay rubbery/fibrous and most often lands at L7 rather than L6. L6 is reached only if the flesh softens enough to squash under the fork while holding 8–15 mm pieces; if it will not, choose a softer food or record the event as unresolved — do not certify it as L6 and do not re-label this media as L7.
