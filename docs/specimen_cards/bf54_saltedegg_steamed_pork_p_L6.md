1. PREP

Weigh 250 g fresh minced pork and one salted egg (≈55 g) on the digital scale; separate yolk from white, mash the yolk with a fork, finely chop the white. Combine pork, yolk mash, chopped white, and 30 ml water; mix by hand until sticky. Shape into a flat patty ≈20 mm thick and steam 12 min until just cooked through (core 75 °C on a probe). Cool 5 min. Portion a 100 g specimen and crumble it by hand into pieces. Size by hand, not by sieve: every piece must measure 8–15 mm in its longest dimension. Check each piece against the 15 mm checkerboard and 100 mm ruler in the capture kit — re-cut any piece over 15 mm with the knife, and discard fines under 8 mm. Do NOT press the crumbles through a 4 mm sieve: a 4 mm screen caps every piece at 4 mm, which is Level 5 (minced & moist), and it can never enforce a 15 mm limit — after sieving, no 8–15 mm piece would exist to test. Do NOT blend. Do NOT add thickener; L6 needs no flow control. Record the mass of the sized specimen and the final yield in grams (target 90–100 g from the 100 g portion).

2. IDDSI TEST

Applicable at L6: fork pressure only (§5.1). Spoon tilt (§5.2) and fork drip (§5.3) are L3–L5 tests and must not be run, filmed, or recorded as pass/fail at L6. No syringe flow test above L3 (§5.4).

Fork-pressure test: place a representative 10–15 mm piece on the plate and press it with the back of the fork, normal thumb pressure, until the thumbnail blanches white; hold 5 s. Pass = the piece squashes flat and does not recover its shape when the pressure is released. Fail = the piece stays intact and springs back (too firm — undercooked), or the piece turns to paste with no 8 mm fragment left (too wet / over-worked).

3. VISUAL QC

Pieces on the fork should look moist and pale gold from the salted egg, with no pink pork showing, and should hold their crumbles without slumping when the fork is held level. Pieces should rest as separate crumbles, not a slurry, and a quick tap should not produce liquid pooling under them. Every piece in frame reads 8–15 mm against the ruler.

5. FAIL MODES

Pieces exceed 15 mm after crumbing — re-cut by hand with the knife and re-check against the 15 mm checkerboard; do NOT re-sieve (a 4 mm screen would take the specimen to Level 5).
Pieces under 8 mm, or a slurry of fines — too small for L6: discard the fines and re-crumble from a fresh 100 g portion; sieving to recover them guarantees L5.
Patty is too wet and pools liquid on the fork — drain on paper towel 30 s, re-weigh, re-film.
Meat is undercooked and springs back under fork pressure — re-steam the intact patty 3 min, cool 5 min, re-crumble, re-test; do not steam the crumbled pieces, they collapse to a mash.
If the crumb will not hold an 8–15 mm piece at all (too wet or too fine to size by hand), the batch cannot reach L6 as a hand-crumbled specimen — re-make from a fresh 100 g portion with 30 ml water and size by hand; do not sieve to force it.
Two consecutive failures: re-make from a fresh 100 g portion.
