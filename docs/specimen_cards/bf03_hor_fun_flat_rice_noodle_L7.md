PREP
Use hor fun as packed; do not blend, do not sieve, do not add thickener or water. Weigh 150 g of blanched sheets straight from the pack onto the capture plate. Keep the noodle mass intact — tear only at natural seams if the bundle exceeds the ruler field. Respect the per-level note: whole sheets as served, reference specimen, no processing.

IDDSI TEST
Fork pressure: press fork tines into the noodle stack until the tines blanch white; the sheets must compress fully and recover their shape when the fork is lifted. Spoon tilt: a heaped teaspoon holds its shape on a level spoon — no flow, no slump. Fork drip: lift the fork clear of the plate; zero drip from the tines or sheet edges. The noodle must look and behave as a solid food that is easy to bite and manipulate, with no separation of liquid or sheet fragments.

VISUAL QC
Sheets lay flat, smooth, glossy, and continuous, with no frayed edges or broken fragments. The stack holds its geometry under the fork. Color is uniform pale off-white with no translucent or mushy patches.

FAIL MODES
Sheets fragmenting into short strips or releasing starch water means over-handling or over-blanched stock — reject, re-blanch a fresh pack. Sheets that bend but fail to flatten on the spoon indicate undercooked texture — reject, re-cook to pack instructions. Any drip from the fork indicates excess surface moisture — drain 30 s on the sieve and re-test; if drip persists, re-make.
