1. PREP
Start with 300 g takeaway sweet and sour pork, sauce removed. Separate the pieces from the sauce. Rinse the pieces briefly (10 s) under running water to strip the glossy starch-thickened coating, then pat dry with paper towel. Strip off and discard the batter crust — it does not soften to a fork-crushable texture at any steaming time. Dice the recovered meat on the 15 mm checkerboard grid to 8-15 mm cubes: each cube must be larger than half a 15 mm cell (≥8 mm) and must not exceed one full cell (≤15 mm). Pick out any fines below 8 mm by hand. Do NOT blend. Do NOT sieve: a 4 mm mesh would destroy an L6 specimen and must never be used to size one. Do NOT add thickener, sauce, water, or any liquid. Record the actual meat yield (expect 180-200 g from a 300 g battered portion). Steam the cubes in a single layer in a steamer basket over 500 ml briskly boiling water for 8 min, then check every 60 s: a cube must crush under thumbnail pressure and not spring back — target window 8-10 min. Plate 100 g of steamed cubes spread in a single layer, not mounded.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork pressure (5.1; the only test that applies at L6), filmed as ONE unbroken take that carries both readings. (a) Softness: place one cube on the fork tines and press with the thumbnail until the nail blanches white; pass = the cube compresses or crushes under that pressure and does not spring back when the fork is lifted. (b) The L6 sizing check that 5.1 requires in the SAME take: hold two or three cubes against the 100 mm ruler; every piece must measure <= 15 mm on its longest edge and no piece may fall below 8 mm. Pass = (a) AND (b). A fork-press take with no ruler read in it is not a passed L6 test — re-film with the ruler in frame.
Spoon tilt, fork drip and syringe flow do NOT apply at L6 — do not run them and do not record them as pass/fail. The 10 ml/10 s syringe-flow criterion is an L3 test only and is withdrawn from this card. There is no bench adjustment (thickener, extra liquid) that moves a solid specimen into or out of L6: the level is set by piece size (8-15 mm) and cooked softness.

3. VISUAL QC
Surface dull and matte, not glossy; no sauce film. Cubes separate, each between half a cell and one full 15 mm checkerboard cell (8-15 mm), no fines below 8 mm and nothing above 15 mm. Fork tines sink into a cube under pressure; the cube deforms and stays deformed (it may split or crush, but it must not mash to a paste). Colour matte mid-brown; no red sauce pooling on the plate; no free liquid on the plate.

5. FAIL MODES
Achievability, stated plainly: takeaway sweet-and-sour pork is battered, deep-fried and sauce-coated. Stripping the batter recovers only part of the portion and the recovered meat is often already over-tender from the sauce braise, so the 8-15 mm soft-but-intact L6 window is narrow and batch-dependent. The procedure above is the closest reliable bench method; if it does not land in the window, reject the specimen and substitute slow-cooked or braised pork shoulder diced to 8-15 mm (reliably L6). Record the substitution on the data sheet.
Hard batter crust left on the cubes — re-strip and re-dice; never blend.
Cubes above 15 mm — re-dice against the checkerboard; never pass an L6 specimen through a sieve.
Cubes below 8 mm after dicing — pick the fines out by hand; a 4 mm sieve cannot be used on an L6 specimen.
Cubes do not crush under thumbnail pressure after 10 min steaming (still firm or crunchy from residual batter) — the specimen cannot be brought to L6; reject and substitute braised pork shoulder.
Cubes mash to a paste under light pressure — below L6 (L5 or finer). Start from a firmer cut and steam for the 8 min minimum. Do not thicken, blend, or add liquid to recover the level.
Sauce residue makes the pieces glossy and sticky so they cohere into a mass — remove all sauce, rinse 10 s, pat dry, re-plate in a single layer; if they still cohere, reject. Do not wash the meat to waterlogging.
