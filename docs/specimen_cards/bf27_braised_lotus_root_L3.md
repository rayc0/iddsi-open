1. PREP
Weigh 200 g braised lotus root. Add to blender with 100 ml cool water; blend 45 s until smooth. Pass through the 4 mm sieve once to remove fibrous strands; discard residue. Measure 200 ml of sieved puree. Add 3 g thickener per 100 ml (6 g total) — this is the L3 calibration starting point; record the actual grams used. Stir in with a fork for 30 s, rest 2 min, stir again. If the puree runs off a spoon, add 0.5 g thickener per 100 ml increments up to a max of 5 g/100 ml; if it sits in a mound that holds shape, dilute with 5 ml water and retest. Per-level note: blend-thin is feasible — sieve the fibres out, then thin with thickener and water to a pourable consistency.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork drip test: spoon a small mound onto the fork tines; the specimen should drip slowly through the gaps in 1-2 drips, not pour and not sit as a solid lump.
Fork pressure test: press fork tines into the specimen on a plate; the tines leave a clear, persistent impression but the specimen does not recover shape. Pass.
Spoon tilt test: tilt a heaped spoon; the specimen flows off in a slow ribbon that leaves a thin coating on the spoon. Pass.
Syringe flow test: fill the 10 ml IDDSI syringe to the 10 ml mark, release for 10 s; pass when 8-10 ml remains in the syringe (i.e., 0-2 ml flows out).

3. VISUAL QC
On the fork: smooth pale beige coating that drips in a slow continuous thread, no visible fibrous strands. On the spoon at rest: a glossy pool that slowly levels but does not run flat instantly, no lumps, no separation of liquid ring around the edge.

5. FAIL MODES
Too runny: pours cleanly through fork tines, syringe empties below 8 ml. Re-make: add 0.5 g thickener per 100 ml and retest.
Too thick: mounds on fork without dripping, syringe retains more than 10 ml. Re-make: add 5 ml water, stir, retest.
Fibrous strands blocking drip: visible threads between fork tines. Re-make: re-sieve through 4 mm and increase initial water to 120 ml, then re-thicken.
