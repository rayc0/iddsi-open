**Bought form & allergens:** bought as a chilled ready-to-eat coconut jelly / coconut milk dessert in a pack or can, eaten cold. Allergens: coconut — treated as a tree nut for FDA labelling purposes; also check the pack for milk, soy, or nut cross-contact.

1. PREP
- Keep the pack chilled until the moment of prep. Weigh 200 g of coconut jelly straight from the pack onto the scale.
- On a clean board with a chef's knife, cut the jelly into cubes measuring 8–15 mm on all three sides — target ~12 mm, checked against the 100 mm ruler. Every cube must be ≥8 mm and ≤15 mm; discard any cube under 8 mm (it reads L5) and re-cut any cube over 15 mm. Discard any cube with a hard skin or set-crust edge.
- Do NOT blend — blending destroys the cube structure and is incompatible with L6.
- Do NOT sieve — the cubes must stay intact, and a 4 mm sieve cannot enforce a 15 mm limit in any case.
- Do NOT add thickener, water, or any liquid — L6 calibration starts dry.
- Record the final specimen mass and the number of cubes produced.

2. IDDSI TEST
- ACTIVE — 5.1 fork pressure. Press the fork tines firmly onto one cube on a white plate until the thumbnail blanches white; hold 3 s; lift. PASS: the cube squashes, does not spring back, and does not return to its original shape with the fork removed. Test 3 cubes; all 3 must pass. If a cube bounces back, re-test on a fresh cube; if it still recovers, chill the pack 30 min longer and re-cut.
- SKIP — 5.2 spoon tilt. Applies at L3/L4/L5 only; L6 cubes must sit on a tilted spoon rather than flow, so the test cannot separate L6 from L7 — the 8–15 mm cube size does that.
- SKIP — 5.3 fork drip. Applies at L3/L4 only; coconut jelly cubes carry no free liquid to drip through the tines, so the test always passes and carries no information.
- SKIP — 5.4 syringe flow. Every L3 only; 8–15 mm cubes cannot be drawn through a 10 ml syringe.
- L6 verdict = all cubes 8–15 mm AND 5.1 fork pressure passes.

3. VISUAL QC
- Cubes hold their shape on the fork, glossy and translucent, edges clean, each 8–15 mm against the ruler.
- The surface looks moist but no free liquid pools on the plate.
- A pressed cube leaves a visible flattened dent that stays after the fork is removed.
- Cubes sit as distinct pieces in a heap on the fork — not a smear, not a single mass.

5. FAIL MODES
- A cube measures over 15 mm: re-cut to 8–15 mm and re-measure against the ruler; re-shoot.
- A cube measures under 8 mm: discard it — it reads L5. Re-cut from a larger block; do not mash the fines back together.
- A cube recovers its shape after fork pressure (rubbery gel): chill the pack 30 min longer, then re-cut and re-test — a firmer gel squashes and stays squashed, which moves it toward L6.
- Liquid weeps onto the plate: drain the cubes on paper towel for 30 s, blot gently, re-shoot.
- The pack sets too soft to cut into 8–15 mm cubes and collapses under the knife: this pack cannot reach L6 by cutting — do not try to firm it with thickener. Note it plainly and use a firmer-set pack.
