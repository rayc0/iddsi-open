1. PREP — Weigh 250 g steamed sweet potato flesh on the scale. Hand-mash with a fork to coarse crumb. Pulse in the consumer blender for 5-8 s to reduce lumps. Press the mash through the 4 mm sieve with the back of a spoon; discard retained fibre. Weigh the sieved mash; sprinkle thickener at 0.5 g per 100 g mash, folding in. Add 10-15 ml hot water per 100 g until the mash coheres when pressed but still looks moist. Hold for 60 s before testing. Record actual grams of thickener and water used on the data sheet. Do not blend to a smooth purée; the per-level note requires visible particles up to 4 mm.

2. IDDSI TEST — Fork-pressure test (L5 required): press the fork tines into the specimen on a flat plate; the sample must squash, the tines leave a clear imprint, and the material must not spring back. Spoon-tilt test (L5 required): load a heaped teaspoon and tilt to 45°; the specimen must sit in a cohesive pile and not slide, drip, or fall off. Confirm with the fork-drip test: lift a forkful 5 cm above the plate; no thin liquid drips from the mash. No flow test required at L5.

3. VISUAL QC — Coarse crumbs clearly visible through the sieve holes; surface glistens with moisture but no free liquid pools around the mound on the plate; the pile holds its shape when the fork is withdrawn, leaving distinct tine marks.

5. FAIL MODES — (a) Over-blended to a smooth purée — fails fork-pressure, recovers like paste; remake, pulse only 5-8 s and sieve. (b) Too dry, crumbs do not cohere, fall off the tilted spoon — add 5 ml water per 100 g and rest 60 s before re-testing. (c) Excess liquid pools on the plate, indicates L4 or lower — fold in 0.2 g thickener per 100 g, rest, re-test.
