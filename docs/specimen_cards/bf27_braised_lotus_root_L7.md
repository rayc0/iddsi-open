1. PREP
Use the braised lotus root as purchased. Do not blend, sieve, or thin. Per the food list, handle as slices as braised. Select one intact slice ~15 mm thick, ~40-50 g, as the reference specimen. Place on the A4 capture mat. Record actual specimen mass on the data sheet. No thickener, no water, no processing of any kind.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork pressure test: press fork tines into the slice until thumbnail blanches white. The slice must compress, deform, or break apart under this pressure — it must not return to its original shape. Spoon tilt test: not applicable at L7 (5.2 covers L3/L4/L5 only) and is not run, filmed or recorded as L7 evidence. Fork drip test: not applicable at L7 (5.3 covers L3/L4 only) and is not run, filmed or recorded as L7 evidence.

3. VISUAL QC
Slice keeps its round, holed lotus-root cross-section with visible cavities. Color is deep mahogany-brown, surface glossy from braise. A light pool of sauce sits around the slice but does not flood the plate. Edges of any break show fibrous, cooked-through texture — no pale raw core. By-eye check only (not a section 5.2 test at L7): lift a portion on a spoon and tilt to 90 degrees; the piece must fall off easily with no flowing liquid and no sticky residue pulling back toward the spoon. By-eye check only (not a section 5.3 test at L7): hold a loaded fork over a plate; no continuous drip should run through the tines; pass criterion for L7: food can be cut, mashed, or broken apart with pressure from a standard fork or spoon, and holds its shape on the plate while sitting in.

5. FAIL MODES
Slice too firm to deform under fork pressure: re-pick a thinner slice from the same batch. Slice collapses into puree under pressure: re-pick and confirm the sample is sliced, not mashed. Excess thin sauce runs through fork tines: drain briefly on paper, re-plate, re-shoot.
