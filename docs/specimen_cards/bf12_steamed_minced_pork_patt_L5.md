Target: IDDSI Level 5 — moist mince, lumps ~4 mm, fork-mashable, holds a shape on the spoon.

1. PREP
Bought form: 150 g fresh minced/ground pork shoulder as sold, about 20% fat, minced once, unseasoned. Seasoning for the whole 150 g: 2 g salt, 0.5 g white pepper, 10 ml cold water, plus 5 g cornflour/cornstarch if a firmer set is wanted. Allergens: none of the major declared allergens as seasoned here (pork, salt, white pepper, water, cornstarch). Do not add soy sauce or oyster sauce — both introduce soy and wheat/gluten.
Mix the mince with the salt, pepper, water and cornstarch and stir 60 s in one direction until sticky. Form into a patty 100 mm across and 15 mm thick on a heatproof plate. Steam over a rolling boil, covered, for 12 min — cooked when a broken piece shows no pink. Cool 2 min. Tip the patty and all released juices into a bowl, then mash and chop with the back of a fork against the bowl wall for 30–40 s, scraping the bowl, until no particle visibly exceeds 4 mm.
Gauge check — the 4 mm sieve is a gauge here, never a production step: take a 20 g sample of the mashed mass and press it against a 4 mm sieve with the back of a spoon. If the sample passes, particles are ≤4 mm and the batch is gauged. If material is retained on the mesh, mash the whole batch a further 20 s and re-gauge. Never press the whole batch through the sieve and never discard what stays on the mesh.
If the mass looks dry to the eye, fold in 5 ml of the reserved steaming liquid, fold 20 s, and reassess; up to 10 ml in total. No thickener in the base prep (corrective use only, see FAIL MODES, ceiling 1.0 g per 100 g). Plate the whole batch — expect 130–140 g after steaming — and record the plated mass.

2. IDDSI TEST
- 5.2 Spoon tilt — ACTIVE, the deciding L5 test. Take a heaped 5 ml teaspoonful and tilt the spoon to 45°. Pass = the mass holds its shape as a cohesive mound and does not slide off, run or drip.
- 5.1 Fork pressure — SUPPORTING CHECK only at L5; per section 5.1 it is a pass/fail test at L4/L6/L7, not at L5. Press the tines flat into the specimen and record whether the mass squashes under thumb pressure and stays squashed with no rebound. Log the result as supporting evidence; it does not decide the level on its own.
- 5.3 Fork drip — skip: applies at L3 and L4 only; a moist L5 mince is scored by spoon tilt, and fork drip has no pass criterion at L5.
- 5.4 Syringe flow — skip: applies at L3 only; an L5 specimen cannot be drawn into a 10 ml syringe.

3. VISUAL QC
On the fork: a cohesive lump sitting as one mass with no crumbs falling away, glinting with moisture, and no individual particle visibly larger than 4 mm against the 15 mm checkerboard. On the spoon: the mound holds its peak at 45°, surface slightly glossy from the meat juices. On the plate: no separate liquid pooling around the mass — a light moist film at most. Colour is uniform pale grey-brown throughout with no pink showing, confirming every particle reached temperature.

5. FAIL MODES
Any particle over 4 mm at the gauge check: mash that portion against the bowl wall for a further 20 s and re-gauge with the 4 mm sieve (gauge only — do not sieve the batch as a production step and do not discard retained material).
Too dry — the mass is crumbly, falls apart on the fork and will not hold a mound on the spoon: fold in 5 ml reserved steaming liquid, mash 20 s, retest; up to 10 ml in total.
Too wet — the mass slides off the spoon at 45° and liquid pools on the plate: spoon off and discard the free liquid, then re-mash; if it is still loose, add 0.5 g thickener per 100 g specimen, fold 30 s, rest 60 s, retest (ceiling 1.0 g per 100 g).
Mass feels firm or rebounds under the supporting fork-pressure check: re-steam — the intact patty for 3 min if caught before mashing, or the mashed mass in a covered dish for 3 min if caught after — then re-mash and retest.
Very lean mince that stays crumbly even with the full 10 ml of liquid: this batch cannot reach L5 by this method — remake with mince of ≥20% fat and note the deviation on the data sheet.
