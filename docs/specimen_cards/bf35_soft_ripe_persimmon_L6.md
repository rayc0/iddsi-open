1. PREP — Stage a firm-ripe persimmon, ripe enough to eat but firm enough to hold a cut edge; fully ripe jelly flesh will not hold an 8 mm piece, and that is the limit of this food. Peel, discard skin and stem, quarter by hand; remove seeds and any fibrous core. Weigh 150 g flesh on the scale, zeroed first. Cut by hand into 8–15 mm pieces measured against the ruler — knife-cut or hand-torn. No blender and no sieving: a 4 mm mesh caps every particle at 4 mm and forces the specimen to L5, and no mesh of any size can enforce a 15 mm limit. Do not add water, milk, or liquid — extra moisture pushes the specimen toward L5. Do not add thickener — L6 does not require thickener; adding it would push the specimen toward L4/L5 and is incompatible with the level. Calibrate scale to zero before weighing specimen for capture. Target specimen 150 g, tested within 2 min of cutting.

2. IDDSI TEST — Fork-pressure test, the only sanctioned test at L6: hold an 8–15 mm piece on a flat plate and press with the thumbnail until it blanches white; the piece must squash flat and not recover its original form when pressure is released — PASS. Confirm the piece measured 8–15 mm on the ruler before pressing; a specimen that is a smear before the fork touches it has not passed anything. Spoon-tilt test: NOT APPLICABLE at L6 — do not run. Fork-drip test: NOT APPLICABLE at L6 — do not run. The 10 ml/10 s syringe flow test does not apply to L6 and should not be run.

3. VISUAL QC — Pieces hold a soft, rounded shape on the spoon without pooling as sauce. Every piece measures 8–15 mm on the ruler; none below 8 mm, none beyond 15 mm. Color is uniform deep orange throughout the pile, no pale fibrous lumps. Surface looks moist but not glossy-wet; nothing runs off the spoon edge when level. The spoon here is a viewing surface only: the L6 level evidence is the fork-pressure film plus the ruler measurement, so do not run, film or record a spoon-tilt result.

A retake takes the next take number; never overwrite a take.

5. FAIL MODES — Underripe fruit: pieces stay springy and recover after fork-pressure — re-make with riper fruit. Pieces slump, merge, or pool as sauce within 60 s of cutting: this food cannot be certified L6 on the bench — ripe persimmon flesh will not hold an 8–15 mm cut edge reliably, and no amount of sieving or blending fixes that (sieving guarantees L5). Record what the specimen actually is — if it spreads into a loose moist mound that does not pour it is L5, if the mass sits on the fork and holds a soft shape it is L4, if it flows off the spoon in a stream it is L3 — and shoot that level's test set instead (L5: 4 mm sieve gauge, spoon tilt, fork pressure as a supporting check; L4: fork drip, fork pressure, spoon tilt; L3: fork drip, spoon tilt, 10 ml/10 s syringe flow). Never sieve or blend an L6/L7 specimen below 15 mm. Pieces cut below 8 mm — re-cut larger. Liquid pooling on the plate after the test: moisture has separated, the fruit is too ripe for L6 — see the re-label rule above; do not sieve to rescue it.
