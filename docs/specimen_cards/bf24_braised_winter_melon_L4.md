1. PREP
Weigh 200 g braised winter melon including its cooking liquid. Tip into the blender; blend 45-60 s until fully smooth. Sieve through the 4 mm sieve using the back of a spoon; discard fibrous residue. Return puree to the blender, add 2 g thickener per 100 ml of puree (record actual grams used on the data sheet). Blend 20 s, rest 60 s, blend 10 s more so the thickener is fully hydrated. Do not add water. The starting mass and final mass are recorded for yield.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork pressure test: press the fork tines flat into the surface; the prongs leave a clear, clean imprint and the puree holds the dent without running back together.
Fork drip test: lift the fork; the puree sits as a mound sitting on top of the tines, not flowing through or dripping in a thin stream.
Spoon tilt test: scoop a heaped spoonful and tilt to a steep angle; the mound must sit on the spoon without sliding off, no separate thin liquid pools on the plate.
Level 4 confirmation: all three must pass; the fork-drip mound above the tines is the deciding cue.

3. VISUAL QC
Surface is glossy, uniform, off-white to pale jade with no fibre specks. A spoon drawn through it leaves a slow-filling groove that holds its outline for several seconds. A fork lifted from the surface carries a soft peak that sits proud of the tines.

5. FAIL MODES
Too thin: puree flows through fork tines or liquid pools on tilt — add 0.5 g thickener per 100 ml, reblend, rest 60 s, retest.
Too thick / gummy: puree cracks or balls under fork pressure — thin with 5-10 ml of the original braising liquid, reblend, retest.
Lumpy or fibrous: residue visible after sieving — re-sieve the full batch, do not patch; if fibre persists, re-blend an extra 30 s and sieve again. Re-make from the recorded base recipe; do not adjust blind.
