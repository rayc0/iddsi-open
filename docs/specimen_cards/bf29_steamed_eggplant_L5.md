**Level 5 — Minced & Moist.** Tests that apply at L5: **spoon-tilt (mandatory)** and **fork-pressure (supporting check only)**. Fork-drip does not apply at L5 and is not run on this specimen.

1. PREP — Weigh 250 g freshly steamed eggplant (skin on, tender; peel away any tough or papery outer strips, as retained skin must be tender enough to mash to about 4 mm). If any piece is larger than 4 mm, mash it with a fork on a plate; if the mash remains uneven, pulse in the blender 5–10 s only — do not purée.

The 4 mm sieve is a **GAUGE** at L5, not a size reducer. Do not press the batch through it and do not discard the retained fraction. Gauge check: put one heaped teaspoon of mash on the 4 mm sieve and press once with the back of a spoon — the mash must break into lumps of about 2–4 mm with no single lump larger than 4 mm; return the gauged sample to the plate. If a lump is larger than 4 mm, re-mash or pulse 2–3 s and re-gauge.

Target yield: about 200 g of soft, moist mash with visible lumps of 2–4 mm, none larger than 4 mm, and no separate liquid pooling on the plate. Add no water, no oil, no thin liquid, no thickener (thickener is not used at L5; this is the L3 calibration field only — leave blank).

2. IDDSI TEST — Spoon tilt (mandatory at L5): place a heaped teaspoon on the spoon and tilt it to ~45°, then to almost vertical. PASS — the specimen sits in a pile, does not slide off, and does not run; no separate thin liquid runs off the spoon.

Fork pressure (supporting check only at L5 — it does not by itself pass the specimen): press the tines of a fork into a heaped teaspoon of specimen on a flat white plate with firm thumb pressure until the thumbnail blanches; the mash must squash completely and stay squashed — no spring-back, no original shape recovered — while still showing visible lumps of 2–4 mm in the squashed mass.

Fork drip does not apply at L5 and is not run.

3. VISUAL QC — On the fork: damp, soft clumps the size of coarse minced meat (2–4 mm), no shiny wet film, no droplets falling between tines. On the spoon: holds a heaped mound when tilted; surface looks matte-moist, not glossy. On the white plate under the specimen: no free liquid ring spreading around the mash.

5. FAIL MODES — (a) Any lump larger than 4 mm at the gauge check: re-mash the retained fraction or pulse 2–3 s and re-gauge; if lumps still exceed 4 mm the batch is too fibrous — discard and start with a softer eggplant. (b) Free thin liquid pools on the plate: the eggplant was over-steamed or watered; drain on a paper towel for 2 min, then re-run the spoon-tilt test. (c) Specimen springs back under fork pressure: it is under-processed — mash by fork or pulse-blend 2–3 s, re-gauge, then re-test; if it has gone smooth and pasty with no visible lumps it has been over-processed to L4, so discard and restart from a fresh 250 g portion, steaming only until just tender. (d) Spoon tilt fails because the mash runs off the tilted spoon: it has collapsed to L3/L4, not L5 — start from a firmer eggplant, steam only until just tender (not collapsing), and mash by hand rather than blending.
