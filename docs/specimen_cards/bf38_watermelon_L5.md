1. PREP
Weigh 200 g of fresh de-seeded watermelon flesh on the digital scale. Do not blend; blending releases thin juice and the per-level note forbids separate thin liquid. Dice the flesh by hand into pieces ≤4 mm using the knife and ruler as a guide; visually confirm no piece exceeds a 4 mm cube. Pour off and discard any pooled juice that separates. Mash with the fork until uniformly minced and visibly moist but with no standing liquid; if the mash looks wet, press gently against the 4 mm sieve to blot excess juice. Do not add water or thickener; for L5 no thickener is used. Note: true L5 watermelon is borderline because the fruit is mostly water; if liquid keeps separating, the specimen cannot meet L5 and must be re-made or downgraded.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork-pressure test: press the fork tines flat onto a heaped teaspoon of specimen; the mass must squash and leave clear tine prints with no elastic recovery. Spoon-tilt test: tilt a spoonful 45°; the specimen holds together as a cohesive mass, with only minimal juice that does not pour. Fork-drip test: lift a fork-load; no thin liquid drips through the tines. All three must pass; any drip of free juice is a fail.

3. VISUAL QC
Coarse mince on the fork, not a purée. Pieces are visible, soft, and glisten with juice but sit in a damp pile, not a puddle. Color is even pink-red; no whole seeds, no translucent lumps over 4 mm.

5. FAIL MODES
Visible pooled juice on the plate or dripping from fork — re-make and blot, or downgrade. Pieces larger than 4 mm after dicing — re-dice and re-check against ruler. Specimen that runs through the 4 mm sieve as liquid — cannot meet L5; record as N/A and discard.
