1. PREP
Weigh 150 g of fresh double-skin milk from the cup. Do NOT blend — the per-level note requires spoon-broken curds, not a smooth puree. Press the cup contents with the back of a fork against the bowl side, working in small scoops until the largest curd pieces are no more than 4 mm. Sieve check (a gauge, not a processing step — L5 is not a lump-free level): weigh out 25 g of the broken curd, rub it through the 4 mm sieve with the back of a spoon, and weigh what is retained. Retained weight must be 1 g or less (4% of the sample); if more is retained, the curds are too coarse for L5 — re-break the whole batch and re-check. Do not sieve the specimen you serve. Check on the fork (supporting check only at L5): a curd pressed by the fork base must squash flat and stay flat when the fork is lifted (no spring-back). If it springs back, keep breaking. No water, no milk, no thickener is added. Record the actual starting weight, the time spent breaking, and the sieve reject weight.

2. IDDSI TEST
Spoon-tilt test (the deciding test at L5): load a level spoonful and tilt it to 45° over 5 s. Pass if it holds together and slides as a clump with no thin liquid pouring off.
Fork-pressure test (supporting check only at L5, not the defining test): press the fork tines into a curd pile on the plate; the curd must squash, the thumb blanches, and when pressure is released the curd stays flat — no recovery, no elastic bounce.
Fork drip is an L3–L4 test and does not apply at L5 — do not run it and do not record it as pass/fail. No 10 ml syringe flow test is required for L5.

3. VISUAL QC
On the fork: matte, off-white curds in distinct pieces up to 4 mm, no shiny liquid pool between them, no skin film. On the spoon: mound holds its shape, surface slightly wet but not glossy, no milk weeping into the spoon well.

5. FAIL MODES
Curd rebounds when the fork is lifted → over-sized or gummy curds; re-break and re-run the 4 mm sieve gauge, do not accept. Free thin milk pools on the plate or pours off the tilted spoon → the set is too loose or the curd was over-worked; do not add thickener to mask it, remake from a fresh cup. Curd breaks down into a smooth paste rather than discrete ~4 mm lumps → the batch reads as L4, not L5; remake from a fresh cup and record the outcome. Glossy skin films the surface → skin not broken through; scrape and re-mash before shooting.
