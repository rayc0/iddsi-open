1. PREP
Weigh 200 g of braised pork belly straight from the pack at room temperature per the per-level note. Keep slices as braised — do NOT blend, do NOT sieve, do NOT add water or thickener. This is the reference specimen: the food as normally served. If the pack is in one large piece, cut a representative slice about 30 mm × 40 mm × 20 mm thick; measure the thickness with the ruler (18–22 mm acceptable). Before use, confirm the slice holds its cuboidal shape under its own weight for 60 s at room temperature — a slice that slumps within that minute is not an L7 slice. Record starting mass and the cut dimensions on the sheet.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork pressure (the only IDDSI test that applies at L7): press a standard metal fork down on the slice with the thumb until the nail blanches white, then lift. Pass = the slice does not mash flat and does not break apart under that pressure: the tines score the surface, the score mark persists and does not fill back in, but the tines do not pass through the thickness; the piece keeps its cuboidal shape and must be cut with a knife or chewed to break down; no liquid runs off it. A slice that squashes flat, breaks into soft crumbs, or slumps off the fork is L6, not L7. Spoon tilt (L3–L5), fork drip (L3–L4) and the syringe flow test (L3 only) do not apply at L7.

3. VISUAL QC
Slice holds its cuboidal braised shape — 30 mm × 40 mm × 20 mm, every dimension ≥15 mm — with visible skin, fat, and lean layers; glossy dark sauce coats but does not pool excessively on the fork. A second look should show no soft collapse when the fork lifts the piece — if it slumps or mashes, it is failing toward L6.

5. FAIL MODES
Slice spreads, slumps under its own weight, or mashes flat under fork pressure → too tender for L7, failing toward L6; re-cut at the full 20 mm thickness from the firmer centre of the pack and re-shoot. If a 20 mm centre slice still slumps or mashes, that pack cannot produce L7 by bench manipulation — record the specimen as L6 and re-shoot from a firmer pack; do not relabel an L6 slice as L7. Sauce runs or pools on the piece → dab with a paper towel before shooting; the slice must not sit in free sauce. Tines fail to score the surface and the slice springs back with no mark → the slice is tougher than the braised L7 reference (under-braised, or cut from a dry part); a thicker cut cannot fix toughness — re-braise longer until the tines score, or take the slice from the middle of the pack.
