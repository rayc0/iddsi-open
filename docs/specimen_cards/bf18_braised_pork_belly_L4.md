1. PREP
Weigh 200 g braised pork belly with its cooking liquor. Load into the consumer blender. Blend 45 s; stop, scrape the wall, blend 15 s more. Press the pulp through the 4 mm sieve with the back of a spoon; discard any skin or fat that will not pass. Return the sieved puree to the blender, add 40 ml of the reserved liquor, blend 10 s. Spoon a 1 g mound of thickener over the surface, fold in, rest 60 s. If the mound slumps under its own weight, add another 0.5 g thickener per 100 ml and re-fold. Record the final grams-per-100 ml used.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork pressure: the four tines leave a clear dent that holds its shape; no puree squeezes up between the tines. Spoon tilt: a heaped teaspoon stays on the spoon when tilted to 45 degrees; it does not slide off in under 10 s. Fork drip: lift the fork; the puree sits as a mound above the tines, nothing drips through the gaps within 10 s. All three must pass.

3. VISUAL QC
Surface is glossy and uniform, no visible meat fibre or fat fleck. Colour is even brown from edge to centre. Mound on the spoon holds a soft peak; on the fork it clings as one lump with clean tine marks.

5. FAIL MODES
Puree flows through the fork gaps → blend another 15 s, sieve again, rest 10 min before retesting. Mound slumps on the spoon tilt → whisk in 0.5 g thickener per 100 ml and re-fold. Grainy texture with visible fibre → re-sieve, do not add water. Any fail: discard, remake from 200 g, record the corrective step.
