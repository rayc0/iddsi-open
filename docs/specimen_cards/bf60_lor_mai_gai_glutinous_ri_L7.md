1. PREP
Weigh 200 g of the steamed Lor mai gai parcel as served (rice, chicken, and any visible leaf/outer wrap removed to expose the rice mass). Do not blend. Do not sieve. Do not add thickener or liquid — L7 is the whole-food reference; the parcel-as-steamed note applies. Keep the specimen warm under a lid until testing (≤10 min).

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork pressure test: press a standard metal fork flat onto the surface with steady thumb pressure. The specimen must hold shape, the tines must leave a clear, persistent indentation, and the fork must not fully cut through the rice mass in one press — this confirms solid-food criteria and rules out L5/L6 spreadability. Spoon tilt test: scoop a forkful onto a spoon and tilt the spoon to a steep angle; the sample must not slide, drip, or flow off. Fork drip test: lift the fork with a loaded forkful and hold for 5 s — no continuous drip stream; only a few grains may detach. No syringe flow test (L3 only).

3. VISUAL QC
Rice grains are distinct and bound only by stickiness, not by sauce pooling. Surface looks matte, not glossy with free liquid. Fork tines leave visible imprints; rice does not smear or ooze between the tines.

5. FAIL MODES
Free liquid pools at the parcel base → specimen reads L6 or wetter, not L7; remake with a fresh parcel and dab surface with paper before testing. Rice mass fully crushes through the fork in one press → too soft, possibly under-steamed or over-sauced; remake with a fresh parcel, longer steam. Grains slide off the spoon on tilt → insufficient sticky binding; remake and re-steam a new parcel.
