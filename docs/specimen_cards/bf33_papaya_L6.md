1. PREP
Select a firm-ripe papaya — flesh that yields to firm thumb pressure but still cuts into cubes that hold their shape and do not exude free juice. Peel, halve, and scoop out seeds. Dice the flesh on a clean board into 10–15 mm cubes, target 12 mm ± 2 mm, measuring each cut batch against the 100 mm ruler. Gauge for oversize only: pass the dice over a 15 mm sieve and re-dice anything retained until nothing is held back. Do NOT pass the papaya through a 4 mm sieve at any point — a 4 mm mesh cannot enforce a 15 mm limit, and nothing in the 8–15 mm L6 range can pass it, so a 4 mm sieve would shred the batch to L5. You need 200 g of diced pieces for the specimen plate. Spread the dice on the board for 5 min and lift off any free juice that collects, so no separate liquid is carried to the plate. Do not blend — pureeing destroys the L6 bite-sized texture. Do not add thickener; L6 is solid pieces, not a thickened liquid. Do not add water.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork-pressure test — the only IDDSI test that applies at L6: press the flat of a fork onto one piece, applying pressure through your thumbnail until the thumbnail blanches white, hold 10 s, then lift. Pass = the piece squashes, changes shape, and does not recover. Size confirmation on the 100 mm ruler: every piece must measure 8–15 mm on its longest edge. Spoon-tilt, fork-drip, and syringe-flow tests are not used at L6 and must not be run or recorded as level evidence.

3. VISUAL QC
Pieces should look moist and glossy with clean-cut faces and intact, not ragged, edges, measured 8–15 mm against the 100 mm ruler; no piece below 8 mm. Pieces hold their shape when lifted on a fork — no weeping juice pooling on the plate or spoon. Colour uniform pale orange, no collapsed or mushy cut faces.

5. FAIL MODES
Pieces recover after fork press — too firm; re-test a riper section. If no section of the fruit passes, this fruit cannot be produced at L6 by bench method: the closest achievable is L5 (fork-mashable, ~4 mm), so re-cut the whole batch to 4 mm, re-test, and file it as L5 — do not file an L6 card against fruit that springs back. Pieces smear or crush to paste — overripe or blended; restart with a firmer fruit, and if the whole fruit is uniformly overripe, record L5 as above. Pieces larger than 15 mm — re-dice and re-gauge on the 15 mm sieve before capture. Pieces below 8 mm — the batch has fragmented; re-cut from a firmer cube, do not capture. Free juice pools on the plate — too ripe or insufficiently drained; lift off the juice and re-check; if it re-accumulates within 2 min, the fruit is overripe for L6 and the batch goes to L5.
