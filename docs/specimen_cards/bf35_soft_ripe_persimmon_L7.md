1. PREP

Select a ripe but firm, unbruised persimmon (Fuyu type): the flesh must be firm enough to require chewing, and a soft, melting or bruised fruit cannot reach L7 by any bench method. Chill the whole fruit at 4 °C for 30 min before cutting — chilling is the only bench lever that firms this flesh and it is the maximum firmness obtainable. Record start mass on the digital scale; target 150–200 g for the reference specimen. Wash, pat dry. Do not peel. Cut the fruit in half along the equator with a clean knife. Keep both halves intact; do not blend, do not sieve, do not add thickener, do not add liquid. The specimen is the halved fruit as normally served. Confirm the per-level note: halves as cut is the correct reference form. Blot surface juice with a paper towel immediately before the shot. Set aside one half for the test, one half for plating.

2. IDDSI TEST

Applicable at L7: fork pressure only (§5.1). Spoon tilt (§5.2) and fork drip (§5.3) are L3–L5 tests and must not be run, filmed, or recorded as pass/fail at L7 — ripe persimmon flesh clinging to a spoon is expected and is not a defect. No syringe flow test above L3 (§5.4).

Fork pressure test: press the tines of a standard metal fork into the cut face of the persimmon half with normal thumb pressure until the thumbnail blanches white. Pass = the flesh does not crush, squash, or flatten, and springs back when the fork is removed with no lasting indentation; the piece requires chewing. Fail = the flesh collapses and stays flattened — that is an L6 texture, not L7.

Size check: with the 100 mm ruler and 15 mm checkerboard in frame, confirm every dimension of the half is ≥15 mm; record measured diameter and height in mm on the capture sheet.

3. VISUAL QC

Cut face shows firm, glossy, translucent orange-red flesh, no bruised or mushy areas, no weeping. Fork tines enter the flesh and it springs back with no lasting indentation. No free liquid pools on the plate — free liquid means the fruit is overripe and the specimen fails.

5. FAIL MODES

Under-ripe fruit: flesh is hard, dry, and astringent and does not read as a regular everyday texture — discard and remake with a ripe-but-firm specimen.
Overripe, bruised, or melting fruit: free liquid runs across the plate and the flesh collapses under the fork. This is NOT acceptable texture at L7 — it fails both the no-free-liquid QC line and fork pressure, and no bench method can firm ripe fruit. Discard, remake with a chilled firm unbruised specimen, and record the rejection on the capture sheet.
Flesh collapses under the fork even after 30 min chilling and firm-fruit selection: L7 is not reliably achievable in this fruit on this line. Record the specimen's actual grade — a half that squashes and does not recover is L6, soft — and report the deviation; do not report it as L7.
If any test is ambiguous, retake with the second half and discard the first.
