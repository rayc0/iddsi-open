1. PREP
Open capture kit. Confirm 100 mm ruler and 15 mm checkerboard are clean and within frame guides. Zero the digital scale with the empty specimen plate. Steam one pumpkin wedge 12–15 min, until a fork tip enters the flesh with light resistance and the wedge still holds a clean edge; drain 2 min on the tray. Weigh out a representative wedge of steamed pumpkin, 80–120 g, as normally served; do not blend, do not sieve, do not add water or thickener. The per-level note directs a wedge as steamed, so preserve skin and flesh geometry intact. Cut the wedge so that its smallest dimension is at least 15 mm, and read it against the 100 mm ruler before it goes on the plate — a wedge thinner than 15 mm is an L6 specimen, not L7. Place the wedge on the white plate, set the ruler along the lower edge and the checkerboard in the upper corner. Calibration starting point: none — this is an unprocessed reference specimen, so the only recorded value is the starting mass of the wedge before any handling.

2. IDDSI TEST
Run the fork-pressure test only (protocol 5.1). The spoon-tilt, fork-drip and syringe-flow tests do not apply at L7 — do not film them as evidence for this specimen. Fork pressure: place the wedge on the plate, press the bowl of the fork down onto it with the thumb until the thumbnail blanches white, then lift. Pass when the wedge squashes readily under that pressure and does not recover its shape. In the same take, hold the wedge against the 100 mm ruler and read it on camera: every dimension must measure >= 15 mm. The fork-pressure result alone cannot separate L6 from L7 — the ruler read does, and both must hold. A wedge that mashes to a puree with no piece structure left, or one that measures under 15 mm, is not an L7 result.

3. VISUAL QC
On the fork: intact wedge geometry, matte orange flesh, defined skin edge, no pool of free liquid at the plate. On the ruler: the wedge reads >= 15 mm in every dimension against the 100 mm scale. Color uniform steamed-orange, no grey or raw patches.

4. SHOTS

Clip 2 — IDDSI test film, the fork-pressure test unbroken, with voiceover noting pass/fail. Files: steamed_pumpkin_L7_take1.jpg / .mp4 for each; increment take number on re-makes.

5. FAIL MODES
Over-steamed wedge collapses to mash under the fork — fails L7; re-make with a shorter steam (10 min) and re-shoot. Wedge does not squash and springs back under thumbnail pressure — fails; re-steam 60 s and re-shoot, do not blend. Wedge reads under 15 mm in any axis — that is an L6 specimen; cut a larger wedge from the same batch or mark the cell FAIL, and never submit it as L7. Free liquid weeps onto the plate — pat the surface dry with paper towel and re-shoot, recording the observation; if the wedge itself is exuding liquid, re-make with a shorter steam. Fork splits the wedge cleanly into crumbs — re-make from a fresh wedge at the pinned 12–15 min steam; a crumbly wedge is a dry or stale pumpkin, not an under-steamed one. If the wedge mashes to puree even at the shortest edible steam, this batch has no honest L7 form — mark FAIL and skip the cell rather than submit an L6 wedge. Each fail requires a new take number.
