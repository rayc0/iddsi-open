1. PREP
Peel and steam 300 g fresh taro until fork-tender (~20 min). Cube into ~2 cm pieces, add 60 ml warm water. Blend in the consumer blender on high for 45-60 s until smooth. Stop and scrape the wall at 30 s if needed. Sieve once through the 4 mm sieve using the back of a spoon; discard fibrous residue retained on the sieve. The target consistency is a soft mound, not a pourable puree. Do NOT add thickener at this stage — L4 taro holds shape from its own starch. If the mound slumps after sieving, work in 0.5 g thickener per 100 ml of puree, blend 10 s, and re-test.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork drip: mound on the fork sits in a clear pile above the tines, does not drip or run through gaps — pass.
Fork pressure: pressing the fork tines into the specimen leaves a clear indentation that does not bounce back fully; the surface cracks slightly — pass.
Spoon tilt: a heaped spoonful holds its shape when the spoon is tilted 45 degrees and does not slide off in under 10 s — pass.
Fork-tine gap check: no puree flows through the 4 mm tine gaps when the loaded fork is lifted and held 5 s — pass.

3. VISUAL QC
Matte surface, no wet sheen. Uniform pale lavender-grey colour with no purple flecks. A peak stays where the spoon drops it.

5. FAIL MODES
Too wet: puree flows through fork gaps or drips — re-blend 10 s; if still wet, add 0.5 g thickener per 100 ml and re-test.
Too stiff: fork cracks the mound cleanly with no give — add 10 ml water per 200 g, blend 10 s.
Lumpy: purple fibre strands visible — re-sieve; if residue still >5% by volume, the batch fails, re-prep.
