1. PREP
Open one ready-to-eat pack of peanut paste into the blender jar. Record starting mass on the scale (target 300 g). Blend 20-30 s on high until completely smooth with no seed or skin grit. Do NOT add water or milk - thinning will push the specimen below L4. Do NOT add thickener - the pack should already hold shape. The per-level note says serve as-is; sieving is not required if the blend is smooth. Press a small spoon-load against white paper: if no oil ring spreads and no solids remain, proceed to IDDSI tests.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork drip test: load a fork, lift it, and hold level for 5 s. Pass = the paste sits as a coherent mound on top of the tines, does not drip or fall through. Fork pressure test: press the flat of the fork into a spoonful on a plate. Pass = the paste deforms but does not separate liquid, holds a clean dent when the fork lifts, and does not run to fill the dent within 5 s. Spoon tilt test: scoop, tilt spoon to 90 degrees. Pass = the paste stays on the spoon, no flow. All three must pass.

3. VISUAL QC
Surface should be matte-smooth with no glossy oil pooling on top. Color uniform tan, no flecks of unblended peanut skin. Mound on the fork should hold its peak when lifted - if the peak slumps flat within 2 s, it is too thin.

5. FAIL MODES
Fail 1 - oil separates and pools on top or leaves a ring on paper: re-blend 10 s; if it persists, reject and re-make from a fresh pack. Fail 2 - paste flows through fork tines or drips within 5 s: it is below L4; do NOT add water to rescue, reject and re-make. Fail 3 - visible seed or skin grit after blending: blend a further 15 s, then re-check; if still gritty, sieve through the 4 mm sieve and re-test.
