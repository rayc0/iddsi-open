1. PREP
Wash and drain 150 g seedless grapes; discard any with skin breaks. Pulse in a consumer blender for 6–8 s total (two 3–4 s pulses, scrape down between) on medium until the skins split and the pulp releases, leaving no whole grapes. Stand 2 min, then decant the free juice into a measure and reserve it. Return 40 ml of the reserved juice per 100 g of drained pulp — about 50 ml for a typical batch — and fork-mash to a uniform coarse texture. Target final specimen 120–140 g of pulp plus returned juice; record actual grams.

Do not sieve to remove material — the 4 mm sieve is a gauge only: press one heaped tablespoon through it; if any fragment is retained, return the whole batch to the blender for a further 2 s pulse and re-check. Nothing is discarded at the sieve.

No water, no milk, no thickener in the base specimen; grapes supply their own moisture at this level.

2. IDDSI TEST
Applicable tests at L5: spoon tilt (5.2) is the level test; fork pressure (5.1) is a supporting check only at L5. Fork drip (5.3) is an L3–L4 method and is not used at L5; syringe flow (5.4) is L3 only.

Spoon tilt — the level test: take one heaped teaspoon (5 ml) on a dry dessert spoon. Tilt to 45° — the specimen must sit as a mound and must not flow or pour. Continue to 90° (vertical) and tap the back of the bowl twice. Pass = the mash releases slowly as a single soft lump and slides off, leaving a thin film on the spoon. Fail = it pours off the spoon before 45° (too thin), or it forms a firm dry ball that does not move after two taps (too thick).

Fork pressure — supporting check only: press the tines into a 1 cm heap on a plate. The specimen must squash, change shape, and not spring back.

3. VISUAL QC
Coarse minced pulp on the fork, clearly wet and glossy from grape juice, holding a soft mound shape. Visible fragments all 4 mm or smaller; no skins still cupping a seed cavity; no whole grapes. On the spoon the specimen mounds softly and slowly settles, not runny. No free juice pooling separately on the plate.

5. FAIL MODES
Too thin — the mash pours off the spoon before 45°: stand a further 2 min, re-decant, and return only 30 ml juice per 100 g pulp. If it still pours, seedless grapes cannot reach L5 on their own juice — either record the specimen as L4 (spoon tilt, fork drip and syringe flow per protocol 5.2–5.4) or re-make with 0.5–1.0 g xanthan gum per 100 g pulp blended in 10 s. Do not label a pouring mash as L5.
Too thick or too dry — the mass crumbles under fork pressure, or sits as a firm ball that will not move on the spoon: stir in 10 ml of the reserved juice at a time and re-mash. If the skins are still intact the batch is simply under-blended: pulse a further 2 s.
Too chunky — a fragment is retained by the 4 mm gauge: re-pulse 2 s and re-check.
Any single failure triggers a re-make from the same 150 g starting weight.
