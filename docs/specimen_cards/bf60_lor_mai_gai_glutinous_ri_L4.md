1. PREP
Weigh 300 g cooked lor mai gai (debone, strip chicken). Add to the blender with 120 ml warm stock at 40–50 °C (chicken or rice cooking water). Blend 45–60 s on high until homogeneous. Sieve through the 4 mm sieve, working the puree through with the back of a ladle until nothing further passes; discard retained solids — L4 is lump-free, so the sieve enforces the level here. Weigh back to confirm at least 250 g of puree.

Return the puree to the blender. Thicken with a xanthan-gum thickener at 1.0 g per 100 ml puree, blend 15 s, and rest 60 s. Record actual grams used. Target final mass 250–300 g, no standing liquid.

Pass window — the single target every branch in section 5 is judged against: the puree must sit as a mound on a fork capping the tines, flatten under thumbnail pressure and stay compressed, and release from a tilted spoon as one soft lump leaving a thin film on the bowl. It must not pour, and it must not sit as a stiff unmoving mound.

2. IDDSI TEST
All three L4 tests apply: fork pressure (5.1), spoon tilt (5.2), fork drip (5.3). No syringe flow test — 5.4 is L3 only.

Fork pressure: mound one heaped tablespoon (15 ml) of puree on a dessert fork; press with the thumbnail until it blanches white. Pass = the surface flattens and stays compressed, no crumbs, no run-through. Fail = the puree squeezes between the tines and runs through (too thin), or the mound stays domed and hard with no compression mark (too thick).

Fork drip: lift the fork 15 cm above the plate and hold 10 s. Pass = the puree sits as a mound above the tines and sheds at most a slow single strand or 2 drops. A continuous stream is a fail (too thin). Zero drip is not the L4 target — that is the L5 criterion, and chasing it over-thickens the specimen out of level.

Spoon tilt: scoop one heaped dessert spoon (15 ml), tilt to 90° and tap the back of the bowl twice with a fingertip. Pass = the puree releases as one soft lump within the two taps and slides off slowly, leaving a thin film about 1 mm on the bowl — the spoon is coated, neither empty nor still holding a mound. Fail = it pours off before 90° (too thin), or it remains a full sitting mound after two taps (too thick).

No separate thin liquid may pool on the plate after 60 s.

3. VISUAL QC
Fork: glossy mound capping all tines, clean spaces between prongs blocked by puree.
Spoon: drops cleanly as a soft lump within two taps, leaving a thin film, spoon near-empty.
Surface: matte-sheen, even colour, no rice grains or chicken fibres visible, no watery halo at the plate edge.

5. FAIL MODES
Too thin — puree pours on the spoon tilt, runs through the tines, or sheds a continuous strand on fork drip: add 0.3 g thickener per 100 ml, blend 15 s, rest 60 s, retest. Do not exceed 1.6 g per 100 ml total.
Too thick — puree sits as a stiff mound that does not release after two taps, or the fork-pressure mound stays domed with no compression mark: thin with warm stock at 40–50 °C, 20 ml at a time (up to 40 ml beyond the 120 ml base), blend 15 s, rest 60 s, retest. Never add thickener to correct a specimen that has already been over-thickened.
Over-thickened batch — the operator chased zero fork drip and the puree no longer releases from the spoon: this is a too-thick failure, not a pass. Thin with warm stock as above; if more than 40 ml of extra stock is needed, re-make from 300 g with 100 ml base stock.
Too coarse — visible rice grains, chicken fibre, or grainy paste after sieving: re-blend 20 s, re-sieve through 4 mm, retest.
Watery halo pools around the mound on the plate: phase separation — blend 10 s longer and rest 90 s before re-test. If still failing, re-make from 300 g with stock reduced to 100 ml.
If the puree still cannot be brought inside the pass window — pouring at 160 ml total stock, or a stiff mound at the thickener maximum — the batch is not usable at L4; re-make from 300 g with 100 ml base stock rather than forcing it with more thickener.
