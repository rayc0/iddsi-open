1. PREP
Weigh 300 g cooked lor mai gai (debone, strip chicken). Add to blender with 120 ml warm stock (chicken or rice cooking water). Blend 45–60 s on high until homogeneous. Sieve once through 4 mm sieve; discard retained solids, weigh back to confirm ≥ 250 g of puree. Return puree to blender; thicken by adding 1.0 g thickener per 100 ml puree, blend 15 s, rest 60 s. Record actual grams used. If after rest the puree flows through fork tines, add 0.3 g thickener per 100 ml and re-blend; record. Target final mass 250–300 g, no standing liquid.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork pressure test: mound puree on fork; press thumbnail until blanching. Surface should flatten and stay compressed — no crumbs, no run-through.
Fork drip test: lift fork 15 cm; puree must sit as a mound above tines, zero drip in 10 s.
Spoon tilt test: scoop puree, tilt spoon 90°; specimen holds shape, slides off as one lump or not at all.
No separate thin liquid may pool on plate after 60 s.

3. VISUAL QC
Fork: glossy mound capping all tines, clean spaces between prongs blocked by puree.
Spoon: drops cleanly as a soft lump leaving spoon near-empty.
Surface: matte-sheen, even colour, no rice grains or chicken fibres visible, no watery halo at plate edge.

5. FAIL MODES
Puree drips through tines or runs on tilt: too thin — add 0.3 g thickener per 100 ml, re-blend, retest.
Visible rice grains, chicken fibre, or grainy paste after sieving: too coarse — re-blend 20 s, re-sieve, retest.
Watery halo pools around mound on plate: phase separation — blend 10 s longer and rest 90 s before re-test. If still failing, re-make from 300 g with stock reduced to 100 ml.
