PREP
Start with 200 g braised lotus root. Press each slice flat with the back of a fork against the chopping board, then chop across into strips no wider than 4 mm, then cross-chop into pieces ≤4 mm. Do not blend; blending turns the soft, fibrous flesh into a uniform puree that masks the L5 minced texture. Transfer chopped material to a bowl and moisten with 30-40 ml of reserved braising liquor so no separate thin liquid pools on the surface. Hold back 1-2 slices intact for the fork-pressure test. No sieve, no thickener, no calibration row needed.

IDDSI TEST
Fork drip: lift 4-5 pieces on the fork tines; they cluster together, almost no liquid drips off. Fork pressure: press fork tines into a single piece until the thumbnail blanches white; the piece squashes and does not spring back. Spoon tilt: tilt a spoonful 30 degrees; the full sample slides off as one cohesive mass, not as separate drips. The intact-slice fork test should give a clean squash with no recovery. All three pass = L5 confirmed.

VISUAL QC
On the fork: individual minced pieces are just visible, no smeared puree. On the spoon: surface looks moist and glossy, not dry or weeping. Pieces hold together when tilted, not falling apart into crumbs.

FAIL MODES
Too dry: pieces look pale and pull apart when tilted; re-moisten with 5-10 ml braising liquor and re-shoot. Too wet: thin liquor pools on the surface; drain off excess and re-test drip. Pieces too large: any piece over 4 mm; re-chop finer and re-shoot. Re-make from fresh 200 g if all three fail.
