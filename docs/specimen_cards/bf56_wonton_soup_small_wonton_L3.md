1. PREP — Weigh 200 g cooked wontons plus 200 ml of their cooking liquid into the blender jar. Blend on high for 30 s, scraping down once, then blend a further 15 s. Press the puree through the 4 mm sieve using the back of a spoon; discard the firmer residue (skins, filling fibre). Measure the sieved volume. Add thickener at 3.0 g per 100 ml of puree as the calibration starting point; whisk in with a fork, rest 60 s, stir, re-measure volume. Top up with hot water 10 ml at a time only if the puree is thicker than the flow-test target. Record the actual grams of thickener used and the final volume on the card.

2. IDDSI TEST — Fork drip: puree should drip in slow, continuous, thread-like strands through the tines, not run as a thin stream and not sit in a mound. Spoon tilt: a 5 ml scoop on the spoon should hold its shape and only flow off when the spoon is tilted nearly vertical. Fork pressure: pressing the fork tines into 5 ml on a flat plate leaves a clear imprint with the puree slowly filling the depression; it does not level instantly. IDDSI flow test: fill the syringe to the 10 ml mark, release, after 10 s read the remaining volume — pass = 8–10 ml remaining in the syringe.

3. VISUAL QC — Smooth, glossy, even beige surface and no visible wonton skin or filling flecks after sieving. Coats the spoon back without running. Drips from the fork in slow ribbons, not drops, not a stream.

5. FAIL MODES — Too runny (syringe < 8 ml, drips as stream): whisk in extra thickener 0.5 g per 100 ml and re-test. Too stiff (syringe > 10 ml, sits in mound, fork leaves no drip): thin with 5–10 ml hot water and re-test. Lumpy with skin or filling flecks visible: re-blend 15 s and pass through the 4 mm sieve again before re-thickening.
