1. PREP
L6 requires pieces 8-15 mm that are soft and squashable, and takeaway fried-rice grains are commonly 5-8 mm, which is L5 territory — so the as-bought container cannot be captured as L6 and the pieces must be built to size on the bench. Weigh 250 g Yangzhou fried rice on the scale. Do NOT blend and do NOT sieve: sieving would force the specimen below the 15 mm limit and destroy the piece structure L6 depends on. Spread the rice on a clean board and, with a knife, cut any piece larger than 15 mm down to size. Working in 30 g portions, press the rice into a 10 mm thick slab with the back of an oiled spoon, keeping rice, egg and diced vegetable in the bought proportions, then cut the slab into 10 mm squares so that every piece measures 8-15 mm in its longest dimension. Verify each piece against the 100 mm ruler and the 15 mm checkerboard: a piece clearly under 8 mm is an L5 particle and must be re-cut or discarded before plating. Scoop a 30 g portion (8-10 pieces) onto the plate. Do not add water or thickener. Record the actual piece size range in mm and the portion mass in grams on the capture sheet.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork-pressure test (the required test at L6): press the fork tines firmly onto a piece until the thumbnail blanches white; the piece must squash, change shape, and not recover. Pass: the tines sink and the imprint stays. Size check, part of the L6 pass: every piece measures 8-15 mm in its longest dimension against the 100 mm ruler. No spoon-tilt test at L6: spoon-tilt applies at L3, L4 and L5 only. No fork-drip test at L6: fork-drip applies at L3 and L4 only. Neither may be used to accept or reject an L6 specimen, and the specimen must not be passed or failed on how it flows off a spoon.

3. VISUAL QC
On the fork: a mixed mound of cut 8-15 mm pieces in which the rice grains are clumped into the pieces, egg is soft yellow, and vegetables appear as small colour specks inside the pieces; every piece measures 8-15 mm on the ruler. On the spoon: the pieces sit in a pile with a small amount of oil sheen only, no loose liquid pool. Pieces should not appear glossy-wet, nor separated, dry and crumbly.

5. FAIL MODES
(a) Pieces measure under 8 mm after cutting, i.e. L5 territory — do not capture; re-cut the portion to 8-15 mm and re-verify against the ruler. If the rice is too crumbly to hold an 8 mm piece together, the batch cannot reach L6 by this bench method: record it as L5 and re-shoot against a card re-levelled to L5 rather than labelling it L6. (b) Pieces over 15 mm, or hard grains and chewy meat that will not squash under thumbnail pressure — cut the oversized pieces down to 8-15 mm and discard any piece still hard or chewy; if more than about 10% of the batch resists thumbnail pressure, re-make with a fresh 250 g portion cooked softer (freshly cooked rice, longer steam, no dried-out reheated rice). (c) Excess oil pooling or watery sauce — too wet; drain on absorbent paper for 60 s and re-test. (d) Dry, crumbly pieces that crumble instead of squashing — too dry; discard the plated portion and re-make with freshly cooked rice, folding 5 ml water per 100 g into the rice before the slab is pressed. Do not re-wet the portion already on the plate.
