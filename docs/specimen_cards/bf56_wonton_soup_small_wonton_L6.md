1. PREP — Weigh 200 g cooked small wontons. Cut each wonton in half with kitchen shears so every piece is ≤15 mm; verify by holding a piece against the 15 mm checkerboard squares. Drain cut pieces in a sieve, reserving 60 ml of the cooking liquid for serving. Place cut pieces on a plate; do not blend — blending destroys the bite-sized pieces required at L6. If a specimen feels gummy or sticky on the fork, dust lightly with 0.5 g thickener per 100 g of pieces and re-test. Per the per-level note: serve the cut pieces drained with a small spoonful (≈15 ml) of reserved broth in a separate cup on the side — never pool the broth over the pieces.

2. IDDSI TEST — Fork-pressure test: press a piece with the underside of a fork until thumbnail blanches white; the piece must squash, change shape, and not spring back. Fork-drip test (on the broth only): a fork lifted from the broth should drip steadily through the prongs, not run in a continuous pour. Spoon-tilt test on a drained piece: a spoonful must hold its shape on a tilted spoon without sliding off. All three must pass; one fail means re-make.

3. VISUAL QC — Pieces on the fork show a clean cut edge with visible filling, mat (not glossy) surface from the dusting if used, and distinct 10–15 mm outlines against the checkerboard. Broth in the cup appears thin, pale, and free-running. No pooled liquid sits under the pieces on the plate.

5. FAIL MODES — Pieces >15 mm after cutting: recut and re-measure against checkerboard. Piece springs back under fork (skin too elastic): steam 1 min then retest; if still elastic, re-make with longer simmer. Pieces collapse to paste under fork (overcooked, no structure): re-make with shorter cook and retest. Broth too thick (coats spoon like L4): thin with reserved hot water and retest drip.
