1. PREP

Take 200 g of whole blanched bok choy heads as bought. Do not blend, chop, or sieve. The per-level note specifies heads as blanched, so keep the leaves and stems intact on each head. Place each whole head directly onto the plate for capture. No liquid, no thickener. Record starting mass on the scale before plating.

2. IDDSI TEST

Fork pressure test: press the tines of a fork firmly into a stem section. The food must visibly compress, flatten, or break under pressure without returning to original shape — confirm it does not spring back. 

Fork drip test: not applicable at L7 (5.3 covers L3/L4 only) and is not run, filmed or recorded as L7 evidence. No liquid must drip or run through the tines. 

Spoon tilt test: not applicable at L7 (5.2 covers L3/L4/L5 only) and is not run, filmed or recorded as L7 evidence. The food must stay in place or slide slowly; it must not flow or pour. 

All three passing confirms L7 Regular.

3. VISUAL QC

Whole intact leaves with visible white stems and green leafy tops, no puree, no mash, no sauce pooling on the plate, no liquid sheen when tilted under the smartphone light. By-eye check only (not a section 5.3 test at L7): lift a head with the fork. By-eye check only (not a section 5.2 test at L7): rest a leaf section on a spoon and tilt to 90 degrees.

5. FAIL MODES

Over-blanching turns stems to mush, failing fork pressure as food disintegrates rather than compresses cleanly — re-blanch a fresh head for shorter time. Pooled water from the blanch bath produces drip test failure — drain and pat heads dry before plating. Cut or separated leaves read as processed rather than whole — re-make with whole intact heads.
