1. PREP — Weigh 200 g cooked white rice into the blender jar. Add 100 ml water. Blend 60 s on high. Stop, scrape the jar wall with a spatula, blend a further 30 s. Sieve through the 4 mm sieve, pressing with the spatula so all puree passes; discard residue. Return puree to blender, add 2.5 g thickener per 100 ml of puree (calibration starting point — record the actual grams used). Blend 20 s to incorporate, rest 60 s, blend 10 s. The food list note says "blend-hold": there is no cooking or steeping step that fits this specimen, so none is recorded.

2. IDDSI TEST — Fork pressure: the tines leave a clear, full-depth imprint that holds when the fork is lifted; the mound does not rebound or drip. Spoon tilt: a level spoonful holds its shape on a tilted spoon, no flow over the edge. Fork drip: a forkful held over the plate sits as a mound above the tines; nothing drips in 5 s. The L3 10 ml/10 s syringe flow test is not the L4 criterion and is not used here.

3. VISUAL QC — Uniform off-white colour, no visible rice grains. Smooth, glossy surface with no separated liquid pooling around the mound. Holds a clean peak when spooned.

5. FAIL MODES — Mound spreads or slumps on the tilted spoon, or puree drips through the fork tines → thin it by blending in 5 g thickener per 100 ml increments and re-test. Liquid pools around the mound after resting → blend a further 20 s and rest 90 s before re-test. Grainy texture or rice specks visible after sieving → re-blend 30 s and re-sieve. Maximum two re-makes per take; if still failing, discard the batch and record the outcome.
