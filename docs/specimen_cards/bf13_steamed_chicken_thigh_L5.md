**Level 5 — Minced & Moist.** Tests that apply at L5: **spoon-tilt (mandatory)** and **fork-pressure (supporting check only)**. Fork-drip and syringe flow do not apply at L5 and are not run on this specimen.

1. PREP
Steam 180 g fresh chicken thigh over boiling water until just cooked through (no pink, core 74 °C). Strip and discard skin, bone, and visible fat. Place 120 g cooked meat in the blender; add 40 ml warm broth from the steaming pot. Pulse 6–8 s, scrape down, pulse 4 s more. Stop and check: the meat must be a coarse, moist mince of distinct granules about 2–4 mm across — not a purée.

The 4 mm sieve is a **GAUGE** here, not a size reducer. Do not rub the batch through it and do not discard the retained fraction. Gauge check: place one heaped teaspoon of mince on the 4 mm sieve and press once with the back of a spoon — the mince must break into granules of about 2–4 mm and pass under light pressure, no single lump may sit on the sieve larger than 4 mm, and no stringy fibre may bridge a hole. Return the gauged sample to the bowl and pick out only any long fibres caught in the sieve. If any lump is larger than 4 mm, pulse the whole batch 2–3 s and re-gauge.

Transfer to a bowl, fold through 2 ml more warm broth with the fork to bring to moist, cohesive mince (not wet, not dry). No thickener required. Record final mass; final portion served for test: 80 g.

2. IDDSI TEST
Spoon tilt (mandatory at L5): scoop a heaped teaspoon of mince, tilt the spoon to ~45° and then to near-vertical. PASS — the mince sits in a mound on the spoon; it does not slide off, flow, or pour, and no separate thin liquid runs off the spoon. FAIL — it slides or runs (too wet / over-processed), or it falls away in dry crumbs (too dry).

Fork pressure (supporting check only at L5 — it does not by itself pass the specimen): press fork tines into the specimen until the tines blanch, hold 5 s. SUPPORTING — the mince squashes, does not spring back, and the thumb leaves a clear print, while the squashed mass still shows distinct granules of about 2–4 mm. If it squashes to a smooth paste with no visible granules, the specimen has been over-processed toward L4.

Fork drip and syringe flow are not applicable at L5 and are not run.

3. VISUAL QC
Mince on the fork shows distinct granules, all visibly 2–4 mm and none larger than 4 mm — not a paste. No long stringy fibres visible when the mound is pulled apart with the fork. Surface glistens but no free broth pools on the spoon or the plate. A light thumb-print stays indented.

5. FAIL MODES
Mince still strings or shreds at the gauge check (fibres bridging the sieve, shreds longer than 4 mm) — re-pulse 2–3 s and re-gauge; if still fibrous, re-cook longer before mincing, or discard the batch. A specimen with visible fibres is not L5.
Mince has become a smooth paste with no visible granules — it is now lump-free and holds a soft shape, i.e. L4, not L5. Discard and restart from a fresh 180 g portion, pulsing in 2 s bursts only.
Specimen weeps free liquid into the cup or pools on the spoon or plate — too wet: drain on the sieve 30 s, re-fold with less broth, and re-run the spoon-tilt test.
Specimen balls up dry on the fork or crumbles off the spoon — too dry: fold in 2–3 ml warm broth and retest.
Any re-make requires a fresh take number.
