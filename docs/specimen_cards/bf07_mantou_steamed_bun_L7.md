1. PREP

Remove one mantou bun from the pack. Weigh it on the digital scale; record start mass, target 60–90 g. Steam covered over briskly boiling water for 6 min — 8 min if the bun is 90 g or larger or straight from the fridge — until the core reaches 70–75 °C on a probe thermometer. Doneness is a temperature, not a softness: do NOT steam "until soft", because softness is the defect at L7, and a bun taken past a 70–75 °C core dents under the fork and fails. Rest uncovered 2 min so surface condensation flashes off. Do NOT blend. Do NOT sieve — an L7 specimen is never reduced below 15 mm. Do NOT add thickener, water, or liquid. Use the bun whole, as steamed. Record final specimen mass in grams on the capture sheet. Set aside on the plate of the capture kit and shoot within 10 min.

2. IDDSI TEST

Applicable at L7: fork pressure only (§5.1). Spoon tilt (§5.2) and fork drip (§5.3) are L3–L5 tests and must not be run, filmed, or recorded as pass/fail at L7. No syringe flow test above L3 (§5.4).

Fork pressure test (primary for L7): press the tines of a standard fork into the dome of the bun with normal thumb pressure until the thumbnail blanches white. Pass = the specimen does not compress, squash, or change shape; it returns to its original shape and shows no fingernail-depth indentation and no spread under the fork, and the piece requires chewing. Fail = any dent that remains when the fork is lifted — that is an L6 texture, not L7.

Size check: with the 100 mm ruler and 15 mm checkerboard in frame, confirm every dimension of the bun is ≥15 mm (a whole bun reads ~60 mm across and ≥30 mm high); record diameter and height in mm on the capture sheet.

3. VISUAL QC

Piece holds its domed bun shape with no slumping. Surface looks evenly matte-porous, springy to the touch, no wet sheen, no liquid pooling on the plate around the specimen. The fork leaves no indentation.

5. FAIL MODES

Bun dents under the fork, stays flattened, or splits instead of springing back — too tender for L7 (over-steamed, or taken past a 70–75 °C core). Correct by SHORTENING the steam: discard and re-make from a fresh bun steamed 4 min to the same 70–75 °C core, then re-test. Do NOT re-steam 2–3 min — steaming only softens the crumb further and moves the specimen away from L7; that inverted branch is closed.
Bun core below 70 °C (under-steamed, doughy centre) — continue steaming in 1 min steps until the core reaches 70–75 °C, then re-test. Under-steaming is a temperature defect, not a texture defect: it never makes a bun dent.
Bun surface wet from condensation or a split leaking steam: liquid pools on plate and would read as free liquid → pat surface dry with paper towel, rest 1 min, re-shoot; do not re-steam.
Bun collapsed from being squeezed in pack: loses domed shape → fails visual QC. Use a fresh bun from the pack, do not reshape.
