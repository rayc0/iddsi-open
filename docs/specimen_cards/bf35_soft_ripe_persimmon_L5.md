1. PREP
Use 150 g ripe persimmon flesh, skin and stem removed. Fork-mash on a plate until smooth, pressing and dragging the tines through the flesh. Work in two halves; scrape both halves together between passes so no chunks hide at the seams. Sieve the mash through the 4 mm sieve using the back of a spoon; discard anything that will not pass. Press retained material once more; if residue still exceeds a pea-sized clump, sieve again. No blender, no added water, no thickener. Expected yield: roughly 130–140 g of uniform pulp at room temperature.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork-pressure test: press the fork tines flat into the specimen until the blanch shows through; the pulp must squashes and not spring back. Fork-drip test: lift the loaded fork; specimen must not drip or run through the gaps. Spoon-tilt test: load a teaspoon, tilt to vertical; the pulp holds together and slides as one lump, leaving only a moist film, no separate thin liquid. If any test drips or recovers its shape, re-mash and re-sieve.

3. VISUAL QC
On the spoon: even orange pulp with no visible flecks larger than a pinhead, soft sheen but no pooling liquid at the spoon rim. On the fork: tissue fills the gaps between tines uniformly; the back of the fork reads as a solid colour with no white specks or strings.

5. FAIL MODES
Visible lumps above 4 mm after sieving — re-mash and re-sieve the full batch, do not pick out specks. Free liquid pooling on the plate — drain briefly on paper; if it persists, the fruit is over-ripe and must be replaced with a firmer ripe specimen. Mash springs back under fork pressure — work it longer; if it still recovers, the fruit is under-ripe and unsuitable.
