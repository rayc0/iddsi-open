1. PREP
Select a firm, just-ripe pear; it must resist thumbnail pressure, because a yielding pear is not L7 material. Rinse, dry, and place on a clean board. Cut lengthwise into 6 wedges; core each wedge by removing the seeds and central stem fibres with a paring knife. Cut each wedge crosswise into pieces 15-20 mm thick, and check every piece against the 100 mm ruler: each piece must measure at least 15 mm in both of its shorter dimensions, since L7 requires pieces ≥15 mm and an 8-10 mm slice is L6. Select 8-10 pieces of uniform size, 120-150 g total, as the reference specimen. Do not blend, do not sieve (an L7 specimen must never be sieved below 15 mm), do not add thickener, do not add water, and do not cut any piece below 15 mm. Serve pieces whole on a white plate as normally presented. Record the actual mass in grams and the piece size range in mm on the capture sheet. Keep the plated pieces chilled until capture; warmth softens flesh and drops it toward L6.

2. IDDSI TEST
Fork pressure is the only IDDSI test that applies at L7. Press the tines of a fork flat onto a piece with normal thumb pressure. The piece must not compress, squish, or change shape; it must retain its full original form. Pass: no deformation and no juice released. No spoon-tilt test at L7: spoon-tilt applies at L3, L4 and L5 only. No fork-drip test at L7: fork-drip applies at L3 and L4 only. Neither is evidence for L7, and neither may be used to accept or reject the specimen. Size confirmation, part of the L7 pass: every piece measures ≥15 mm in its two shortest dimensions against the 100 mm ruler.

3. VISUAL QC
Pieces hold their shape on the fork with sharp, intact edges. Flesh surface looks matte and fibrous, never glossy or wet. No pooling liquid visible on the plate beneath the pieces. Every piece spans at least 15 mm on the ruler.

5. FAIL MODES
L7 is NOT reliably achievable with fresh pear on the bench: ripe pear flesh yields to thumb pressure and weeps juice, so the same fruit reads L6 (8-15 mm, soft) on one day and L7 on another, and nothing but the fork test tells the operator which. The closest achievable procedure is the one written above — firm, just-ripe fruit, cut 15-20 mm, chilled, captured immediately. (a) Pieces compress, squash, or release juice under fork pressure — the fruit is too ripe or too warm, so the specimen is L6 and not L7: re-shoot with a firmer, chilled pear; if firm fruit cannot be sourced, re-level the card to L6 and cut to 8-15 mm rather than labelling an L6 specimen L7. (b) Pieces measure less than 15 mm — too thin for L7; re-cut at 15-20 mm thickness, never thinner, and re-verify every piece against the ruler. (c) Juice pools on the plate from bruising or warming — pat pieces dry, chill, and re-shoot; weeping flesh means the fruit is past L7 firmness and the batch must be re-sourced, not re-labelled.
